d# Vaelith

The Lone Star drops out of the Depths into a system with a brown dwarf star that barely qualifies. Dim rust-light touches everything. There is no planet here — just a field of debris, shattered rock and ancient hull-metal tumbling in slow procession around a central absence.

The absence is the catalyst. A hollow sphere of nothing roughly three kilometers across, ringed by a perpetual cyclone of dust, ice crystals, and charged particulate that spins fast enough to glow at the edges. Sensors say the sphere is vacuum. Sensors also say air is moving inside it at seven hundred kilometers per hour in organized laminar layers that should not exist without walls to contain them.

Vaelith doesn't look like a god's corpse. It looks like a god's last breath, caught in a bottle that isn't there.

The cyclone wall is the hard part. Threading the ship through isn't an option, but a shuttle with a good pilot could ride the laminar gaps between shear-bands and punch through into the eye. Inside, the wind is paradoxically still. A sphere of perfect calm surrounded by fury. Suspended at the center is a crystalline lattice of frozen air — Vaelith's lungs, or what's left of them — glinting like spun glass in the dim light.

3 - How did this god or goddess die?
Vaelith was a lesser wind-god — a sky-dancer who served as courier and scout for a greater air-titan during the old wars. When the titan fell, Vaelith tried to carry its dying message across the Depths. The message was too heavy. It was a compressed scream of divine will, and it tore Vaelith apart from the inside out. What remained was Vaelith's lungs and the message still rattling around inside them, generating an endless cyclone of pressurized grief in a debris field that used to be the relay station where Vaelith finally burst.

4 - What magical materials were created?

Breathglass shards: fragments of Vaelith's crystallized lung-lattice. Transparent, impossibly light, and resonant — they hum at frequencies that interact with air magic. Can be ground into lenses for wind-scrying or socketed into a focus to reduce the cost of air spells by one mana.

Shear-dust: the charged particulate spinning in the cyclone wall. When collected and compressed it becomes a powder that, when released, generates a localized burst of cutting wind. Useful as a grenade component or trap reagent.

Courier's Thread: thin filaments of condensed air-magic found trailing from the lattice like spider silk. They transmit sound perfectly over long distances when strung taut. Used in communication rigs and alarm wards.

5 - Does the Catalyst have a Guardian?

Yes — **The Screaming Relay**.

    Form and nature: not a creature in the traditional sense. The divine message Vaelith was carrying has developed a kind of sentence over millennia of echoing inside the lung-lattice. It manifests as a localized sonic weapon — a moving knot of compressed sound that can shatter armor, rupture eardrums, and scramble comms. When it "speaks" it uses fragments of the dead titan's voice, which can stun or paralyze those who hear divine speech unprepared.
    Role and temperament: territorial but not malicious. It circles the lattice in predictable patterns and only attacks things that disturb the air inside the eye. If you can match its resonant frequency (via magic, tech, or a wyrm-bone whistle node) it treats you as part of the relay network and ignores you.
    Strength: appropriate for a Divinity 5 Catalyst — dangerous but beatable. A well-prepared squad with sound-dampening gear and a mage who can manipulate air can neutralize or bypass it.
    Hook: the Relay's patrol pattern shifts every few hours. Timing your approach is half the challenge; the other half is not panicking when a dead titan starts screaming at you through a megaphone made of god-lung.

6 - What are the dominant primal types?

    Primary: Air-primals — pressure-wisps and shear-sprites that ride the cyclone wall and feed on the kinetic energy. Small, fast, and territorial. They attack by creating localized vacuum pockets or redirecting shear forces.
    Secondary: Sound-primals — resonance echoes and harmonic phantoms born from the divine message bouncing around for millennia. They disorient, confuse, and can mimic voices or ship communications.
    Tertiary/associated types: Dust-constructs — loose aggregations of shear-dust and debris that form temporary bodies when agitated. Slow but abrasive, and they dissolve back into the cyclone when destroyed.
