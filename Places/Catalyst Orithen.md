# Orithen

The system has a young yellow star, too bright for the neighborhood. One planet sits in the habitable zone — small, temperate, unremarkable from orbit except for one thing: the entire southern continent is a single organism.

It's a forest, technically. But the canopy is one connected membrane of bioluminescent tissue stretched between trunk-pillars that pulse with a slow, visible heartbeat. Rivers of golden sap run on the surface like veins, pooling in clearings that glow warm amber at night. The air is thick, sweet, and wrong — oxygen-rich to the point of danger. Fires here would be catastrophic.

Orithen is buried at the root-heart, three kilometers below the surface where the trunk-pillars converge into a single massive taproot system. The taproot wraps around the remains of the god like fingers cradling an egg: a fossilized humanoid form no larger than a person, curled fetal, covered in bark and threaded through with living root-matter. It looks like it's sleeping. It isn't.

Reaching the root-heart means descending through progressively denser layers of living wood, symbiotic fauna, and root-guardians that treat anything not part of the organism as an infection to be walled off and digested.

3 - How did this god or goddess die?
Orithen was a lesser life-god — a wandering healer who traveled between worlds mending ecosystems damaged by divine wars. It arrived at this world to repair a biosphere scorched by a stray void-strike and poured too much of itself into the soil. The world healed. Orithen did not. Its body calcified where it knelt, and the ecosystem it was trying to save grew over and through it, using the god's residual life-magic as a permanent engine. Orithen didn't die violently — it simply gave until it was empty, and the world swallowed what was left.

4 - What magical materials were created?

Godwood: the trunk-pillars and root structures closest to Orithen's body are infused with divine life-magic. Harvested carefully, godwood is self-healing — minor cuts and gouges in equipment made from it slowly close over days. Used for ship hull patches, shield cores, and prosthetic frameworks.

Amber Sap: the golden sap that runs on the surface. When refined it acts as a powerful healing reagent and universal blood-substitute for biological life. In its raw form it can be used to stabilize dying tissue or preserve organic samples indefinitely.

Root-thread filament: thin, flexible strands of living root-matter that can be woven into fabric or cable. It responds to life-magic by contracting or relaxing, making it ideal for responsive armor linings, tourniquets, and bio-reactive sensor meshes.

Heartbark resin: dense, dark resin harvested from the innermost layers near the root-heart. When applied to wounds it accelerates natural healing dramatically and prevents infection. Alchemists use it as a base for life-potions.

5 - Does the Catalyst have a Guardian?

Yes — **The Undergrowth**.

    Form and nature: a vast root-network intelligence — not a single creature but a distributed consciousness that operates through the entire forest-organism. It communicates through tremors in the root system, pheromone clouds, and direct neural contact via root-tendrils. When it needs a body, it assembles one from wood, bark, and compacted soil — a towering humanoid shape that moves with surprising speed for something made of lumber.
    Role and temperament: protective and patient. It does not attack immediately — it walls off intruders, redirects them, and tests their intent through escalating environmental pressure (thickening air, closing paths, root-snares). It only becomes violent if you start cutting or burning, at which point the entire forest becomes hostile at once.
    Strength: appropriate for a Divinity 5 Catalyst — dangerous in its home territory but limited in raw power. It fights through terrain control and attrition rather than direct force.
    Hook: you can negotiate with the Undergrowth. It understands the concept of symbiosis. If you can demonstrate that you intend to give something back (plant, heal, restore) it may allow passage. Brute-forcing your way to the root-heart turns a three-kilometer walk into a siege.

6 - What are the dominant primal types?

    Primary: Life-primals — bioluminescent sprites, root-walkers, and sap-elementals that maintain the forest-organism. They heal, grow barriers, and overwhelm intruders through rapid overgrowth rather than direct combat.
    Secondary: Nature-primals — symbiotic fauna that have been living in the forest for so long they've become semi-magical. Insectoid swarms, fungal colonies, and predatory vine-beasts that blur the line between animal, plant, and primal.
    Tertiary/associated types: Bark-constructs — humanoid or beast-shaped bodies assembled by the Undergrowth from deadwood and compacted root-matter. Used as enforcers and heavy labor.
