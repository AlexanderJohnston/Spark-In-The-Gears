# Gronthar

The asteroid field is dense and wrong. Rocks the size of cities, but they aren't tumbling — they're *locked*, held in place relative to each other like vertebrae in a spine that someone straightened and pinned. Your nav officer calls it a lattice. Your sensors call it impossible. The rocks are mostly iron and silicate, but threaded through every one of them are veins of something that reads as mineral but behaves like muscle: dark striations that contract faintly on a twelve-hour cycle, pulling the asteroids a fraction closer together and then relaxing.

At the center of the lattice sits the largest rock. It isn't an asteroid. It's a fist.

Gronthar's fist, specifically — a clenched stone hand roughly two kilometers across, severed at the wrist, still gripping something. The fingers are cracked and scarred, and between them leaks a dull amber glow: the remnant of whatever Gronthar was holding when it died. The rest of the body is gone, shattered across the lattice. Each asteroid is a piece — a chunk of divine stone-flesh locked in formation by residual earth-magic that refuses to let the body scatter.

Landing on the fist is straightforward. Getting inside to whatever it's holding is not. The stone resists cutting, drilling, and blasting with a stubbornness that feels personal.

3 - How did this god or goddess die?
Gronthar was a lesser earth-god — a stone-sage who served as a living fortification for other gods during the old wars. Its body was the wall, its fists were the gates, and its voice was the foundation. When the battle line finally broke, Gronthar was ordered to hold a chokepoint alone while greater gods retreated. It held for long enough. Something eventually shattered it — accounts vary, but the blast was strong enough to scatter its body across a system. Its fist, still clenched around the standard it was ordered to hold, remained intact. The other pieces locked into formation around it, held together by the same stubborn earth-magic that kept Gronthar standing when everything else ran.

4 - What magical materials were created?

Gripsite: the dark, striated mineral-muscle threaded through the asteroids. When harvested and forged it creates alloys that resist deformation under extreme stress. Used for structural reinforcement, blast-resistant plating, and melee weapon cores that don't bend or chip. Equipment made with gripsite feels heavier than it should, but holds.

Hearthite ore: a warm, amber-veined stone found in highest concentration near the fist. It radiates a faint, comforting heat and is naturally resistant to cold and void exposure. Smelted into armor it provides modest thermal regulation. Alchemists grind it into a powder used as a reagent for earth-wards and fortification spells.

Siege Dust: powdered stone-flesh scraped from Gronthar's knuckles. When packed into munitions or thrown as a cloud, it hardens on contact with magic into a dense crystalline shell. Used for breaching charges that create their own shrapnel, or defensive grenades that create instant cover.

Resolve Thread: fine metallic fibers found in the tendons of the fist, still under tension after millennia. When woven into cable or rope it becomes nearly unbreakable under load — the more force applied, the stronger it holds. Used for tow cables, anchor lines, rappelling gear, and prisoner restraints.

5 - Does the Catalyst have a Guardian?

Yes — **The Garrison**.

    Form and nature: not one creature but a squad — seven stone-primals who have taken on humanoid soldier forms over millennia of standing watch over Gronthar's fist. They are roughly human-sized, made of compressed gripsite and hearthite, and they move with military precision. Each one carries a weapon shaped from fist-stone: maces, shields, and one carries a banner made of resolve thread that seems to be a replica of whatever Gronthar was holding.
    Role and temperament: disciplined and territorial. They patrol the fist's surface in rotating shifts and challenge intruders with a ritual demand (a rumbling vibration pattern through the stone that loosely translates to "state your business"). If you answer with hostility or start mining without permission, all seven attack in coordinated formation. If you answer with respect or demonstrate martial discipline, they may grant passage — they respect soldiers.
    Strength: appropriate for a Divinity 5 Catalyst — individually each is a serious threat, and together they fight like a trained unit. Beatable by a well-armed squad, but costly if you're sloppy.
    Hook: the Garrison has been waiting for orders that will never come. They respond to command authority. If someone with genuine military bearing gives them a stand-down order with enough conviction, there's a chance they'll comply — old habits from a god who valued chain of command above all.

6 - What are the dominant primal types?

    Primary: Earth-primals — stone-soldiers, iron-crawlers, and gripsite golems that patrol the asteroid lattice. They are slow, tough, and fight in formation. They don't chase — they hold ground and punish anyone who steps onto it.
    Secondary: Metal-primals — ore-sprites and vein-runners that flow through the mineral-muscle striations between asteroids. Fast within stone, useless in open space. They sabotage equipment by bonding with metal components and locking them in place.
    Tertiary/associated types: Dust-swarms — clouds of siege dust that form semi-solid shapes when agitated. They act as living minefields across the lattice, detonating into hardened shrapnel when disturbed.
