# Kumoshi

As the ship comes out of the Depths and your instruments stop lying to you (as much), you see Kumoshi the way you *feel* it first.

A frozen planet turning slow in a heavy dark. Craters. Old impact scars. No atmosphere worth the name. And yet the space around it behaves like weather.

Wrapped around the whole sphere is the **coil**. An ancient air-wyrm’s remains, spiraled like a crown and a cage at the same time. Rib arcs the size of cities. Cartilage tunnels knotted together. Lung-sacs flash-frozen into translucent ice that catches distant light and throws it back as pale aurora. Frost-dust and glittering debris stream sideways between the ribs as if something is pushing it.

Your sensors read safe pockets that blink into shear. Calm lanes that rotate a fraction while you watch. Trajectory math that comes out correct and still feels wrong. The closer you get, the more it becomes obvious.

Kumoshi doesn’t have air.

Kumoshi has **Breath**.

3 - How did this god or goddess die?
Kumoshi was an ancient air-wyrm of god-fragment scale. In the old war it wrapped itself around a dead world like a tourniquet, trying to seal a wound in reality (or trying to keep something *in*). It succeeded, and then it froze.

Its Breath did not dissipate. It condensed into a pressure ecology inside its own lungs and marrow, turning vacuum into directed force and storm-charge. What remains is a planet in shadow and a spiral reef of wyrm-bone and frozen lung-ice that behaves like a living storm system without an atmosphere.

4 - What magical materials were created?

Frost-lung membrane sheets: pressure-rated, self-sealing when cold. In plates it behaves like an intelligent gasket. Perfect for suit patches, airlocks, emergency bulkheads, and anything that needs to hold “impossible pressure.”

Gale cartilage struts: ultralight structural material harvested from the coil’s inner ribs. It holds shape under torsion like it remembers being part of a flying thing. Excellent for drones, articulated armor plates, wing spars, and EVA rigs.

Storm-sinew filaments: conductive threads that *want* to carry charge. When braided into wards or circuits they dump static safely or weaponize it, depending on inscription.

Hoarfrost marrow: pale alchemical slurry found where the coil’s marrow froze mid-flow. It’s a coolant and a spell-focus reagent. Used to prevent matrix overheating during sustained casting.

Wyrm-bone whistle nodes: resonant “tone keys” grown along the cartilage ducts. When sounded (or energized) they open/close certain vents and redirect shear lanes. A literal navigation key inside the structure.

5 - Does the Catalyst have a Guardian?

Yes — **The Breathkeeper**.

    Form and nature: an Air-Guardian that isn’t fully awake until provoked. It manifests as pressure handprints on ice, words formed in frost, and a silhouette made of compressed darkness and storm-spark. When it fully manifests it becomes something like an elder wyrm’s shadow made of wind.
    Role and temperament: sentinel and steward. It preserves the lungs, punishes reckless harvesting, and tests intruders to see if they are hunters or butchers.
    Strength: appropriate for a Divinity 10 Catalyst — elder-wyrm tier when roused, backed by storm-primals and pressure-constructs.
    Hook: it prefers tests and oaths first. If you act like you are here to strip-mine a god, it escalates fast.

6 - What are the dominant primal types?

    Primary: Air-primals — gale hunters, pressure-wisps, “breath knots” that ride shear lanes. They excel at displacement, seal attacks, and environmental kills.
    Secondary: Storm-primals — static-arc spirits, ice-spark predators, charge-fed gliders that graze along the ribs.
    Tertiary/associated types: Bone/ice constructs — cartilage sentinels, membrane-ward drones, and lung-sack traps that behave like living bulkheads.
