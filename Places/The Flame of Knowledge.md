# THE FLAME OF KNOWLEDGE

The Web of Divinity, the Swarm, the Archive,  
and a Table-Ready Guide to the Derelict Great Ship

**A spoiler-heavy GM reference for Chris Fox’s Magitech Chronicles**

Naming note: the canon location is generally called the Flame of Knowledge, not the Word of Knowledge. The Word of Xal is a different Great Ship. This guide uses Flame of Knowledge throughout.

Core premise: the Flame is a colossal divine archive built around an artifact that can inspect possible realities. Its caretakers were broken by the darkness and insanity surrounding that artifact. The ship is therefore both a library of impossible value and a psychic quarantine zone.

## Canon Confidence Key
- Confirmed canon: directly stated in the official RPG material or Chris Fox’s World Anvil articles.
- Canon-adjacent: promotional descriptions or first-person summaries that may simplify, joke, or use an in-universe narrator.
- GM adaptation: original connective material included to make the location playable. These sections are explicitly labeled.

# 2. Identity and History

## The Ship’s Name

The vessel is the Flame of Knowledge. The similarly named Word of Xal is another Great Ship: a void-aspected warship and world-breaker associated with Xal and Inura. Mixing the two names is easy, especially because both belonged to the Vagrant Fleet and both are explored in the Magitech Legacy era.

## Who Built It?

Two official presentations coexist. The RPG and World Anvil rules text describe the builder as a forgotten goddess whose name was deliberately erased from history—someone who effectively vanished under magical witness protection. Chris Fox’s promotional description of the ship identifies that goddess as Neith and says she created the Flame to house the Web of Divinity. For a tabletop campaign, the cleanest reconciliation is that Neith’s identity was erased from ordinary history, while rare divine or author-level records still preserve the truth.

GM use: discovering the name Neith can itself be a major revelation, a forbidden scale, or the reward for repairing part of the Archive.

## The Dragonflight Era

The Flame served during the age of the dragonflights, when gods, Wyrms, divine officers, and Great Ships shaped interstellar war. Its primary role appears to have been observation, preservation, and strategic knowledge rather than simple frontline combat. Knowledge scales aboard the ship record maps, schematics, Catalyst locations, and battles from that era.

## The Fall of the Vagrant Fleet

The Great Ships were marooned amid a catastrophic battle approximately ten thousand years before the present. Cindara remembers the battle. The Flame now lies derelict within the Vagrant Fleet’s remains, surrounded by debris, lurkers, damaged defenses, and other ancient vessels.

## The Relic-Hunter Expedition

In the Magitech Legacy novel Hatchling, Jerek and his companions explore the Great Ships. Official RPG commentary summarizes several consequences of the Flame expedition: Jerek encountered the Swarm, arachnidrakes, and Cindara; recovered a galaxy-wide Catalyst map accurate to roughly ten thousand years earlier; used the Web’s temporal systems to bring a ship ten thousand years forward in time; and helped an arachnidrake named Kek take up the mantle of Guardian in an attempt to restrain the spreading madness. Kek feared that the corruption would eventually consume him.

# 3. The Web of Divinity

## What It Is

The Web of Divinity is an Artifact with a capital A: a divine-scale construct housed aboard the Flame and connected to the ship’s temporal matrix. It allows a user to examine possibilities—described as perceiving or perusing all possible realities, including every reality in which a particular spider or thread of events exists. It is not merely a database. It is a mechanism for looking across branching outcomes.

## Physical Presentation

Canon establishes its placement more clearly than its exact appearance. The Web is associated with the bridge, suspended above the ship’s fire Catalyst, and accessed through the bridge’s temporal matrix. The Swarm has webbed off the bridge and tries to keep intruders away. This creates a layered visual: a divine web or lattice above a blazing well, surrounded by banks of temporal machinery and drowned beneath ordinary silk spun by billions of corrupted caretakers.

## What It Can Do
- Survey possible timelines and outcomes.
- Reveal historical or alternate events inaccessible through ordinary records.
- Interact with the temporal matrix strongly enough to displace a vessel by ten thousand years.
- Support strategic divination on a scale far beyond normal augury.
- Potentially restore or interrogate records tied to the Archive and its knowledge scales.

## The Cost

Using the Web erodes sanity. The longer someone remains connected, the worse the damage becomes. Official descriptions also refer to a darkness trapped aboard the ship—something eager to spread through the opening that temporal perception creates. The Web does not offer consequence-free time travel or omniscience. Every use risks corruption, obsession, false certainty, and the possibility that something on the other side notices the user.

## Table-Ready Connection Procedure (GM Adaptation)
1. Attunement: the user touches or enters the temporal matrix while the fire well is active.
2. Selection: the user states a question, event, person, object, or desired outcome.
3. Branching vision: the Web presents many answers at once, not a single prophecy.
4. Price: each additional layer of certainty inflicts stress, madness, corruption, or a temporal complication.
5. Exit: the user must consciously choose one reality as 'home' before disconnecting.

Useful rule: the Web answers accurately, but never safely. It may show the correct future while destroying the user’s ability to distinguish that future from the present.

# 4. The Archive and Knowledge Scales

## What Knowledge Scales Are

The Flame’s knowledge is stored on dragon scales—physical scales used as durable magical data media. Official material describes scales containing maps and schematics from the dragonflight age, records of battles, and a map of every known Catalyst in the galaxy as it stood ten thousand years ago. Elsewhere in the setting, knowledge scales are valuable enough that the Confederacy pays bounties for them.

## What Kind of Scales Are Aboard?

Canon calls them dragon scales and does not publish a taxonomy for the Flame’s collection. The safest reading is that they are preserved or prepared scales inscribed with magical records, rather than loose currency-like dragonscales. Their likely contents include stellar maps, Catalyst registries, ship schematics, battle recordings, divine research, personnel records, and alternate-history observations captured through the Web.

## How Many Are There?

No exact number is stated in the public RPG or World Anvil material. The descriptions say that knowledge scales are 'everywhere' and that most are stored in the Archive. That supports an enormous collection—potentially millions—but any precise figure would be a GM invention. For play, treat the Archive as effectively inexhaustible while making intact, indexed, relevant scales rare.

## Suggested Archive Categories (GM Adaptation)

| **GM category**       | **Typical contents**                                        |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **White scales**      | Navigation, astronomy, and Catalyst maps                    |
| **Black scales**      | Forbidden divine histories, erased gods, and sealed threats |
| **Copper scales**     | Engineering schematics and ship systems                     |
| **Red scales**        | Battle records, weapons tests, and fire-aspected research   |
| **Iridescent scales** | Web-derived possibility records and alternate timelines     |
| **Bone-pale scales**  | Personnel, genealogy, oaths, and Guardian succession        |

# 5. Inhabitants and Factions

## The Swarm

The Swarm is a single distributed intelligence expressed through millions—or, in later GM-facing material, billions—of spiders. It was probably created to tend the Web and Archive. The madness aboard the Flame corrupted its caretaking purpose into violent quarantine. It webs off the bridge and attempts to destroy or drive away anyone who approaches.
- Can form faces to communicate.
- Can form prehensile masses or spider-tentacles powerful enough to threaten ships.
- Is often distracted or distributed across the vessel, allowing brief windows for exploration.
- Leaves increasing web density as a reliable warning that intruders are nearing a defended region.
- Produces chitin shards charged with fire magic; its silk is exceptionally strong and heat-resistant.

### How to Portray the Swarm

Avoid treating it like a bag of individual monsters. It is the room, the walls, the surveillance system, and the pursuing antagonist at once. A face may assemble from thousands of bodies. A question may be answered by spiders arranging themselves into letters. A damaged corridor may stand because the Swarm is physically holding it together.

## Arachnidrakes

Arachnidrakes resemble dragons or dragonkin with eight limbs and eight eyes. Many carry staves, use fire magic, and speak ancient draconic. They maintain a tribal society and appear to revere their strongest mages. They are deeply hostile to outsiders, but some scholars will speak before attacking.

## Kek, the Guardian

Kek is an arachnidrake whom Jerek helped become Guardian. The role was meant to contain the ship’s madness, but Kek believed the burden would eventually consume him and require a successor. His current condition is deliberately open. He can be a tragic ally, a corrupted boss, a dying oracle, or the campaign’s central rescue objective.

## Cindara and the Cinder Hatchlings

Cindara (also rendered Cindra in one official summary) is an ancient Wyrm who remembers the battle that stranded the fleet. She and her hatchlings can be left alone if treated carefully. Cindara has largely surrendered to apathy and spends her time playing Arena; her children patrol the ship and tend to attack intruders. Arena software or downloadable content is established as a viable barter item.

# 6. The Catalyst and Its Rewards

## Gaze of Divinity

The Flame’s defining ability is Gaze of Divinity. A mage using it perceives the true nature of things, sees enchantments, looks through barriers and darkness, and senses deliberate lies as a faint buzzing. It does not expose a falsehood repeated in good faith. Acquiring the power also opens a crack in the user’s mind, producing the persistent feeling that shadows are watching.

## Fire Silk

Fire silk is extraordinarily strong, light, and resistant to heat. In the RPG rules it can be woven into clothing or used to embroider sigils, providing 2 armor and 2 fire resistance.

## Chitin Shards

The carapace of a Swarm spider can retain fire magic. Crushing a shard releases that energy for a mage to absorb as spell power. Harvesting it is profitable, dangerous, and ethically complicated if the Swarm can be cured.

# 7. Suggested Expedition Structure

The following zone structure is a GM adaptation built from confirmed locations, hazards, and inhabitants.

| **Zone**                    | **Purpose and pressure**                                                                          |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **1. Debris Approach**      | Thread through the Vagrant Fleet, evade lurkers, identify a viable docking wound.                 |
| **2. Burned Docking Spine** | Vacuum breaches, dormant defenses, strands of fire silk used as emergency structural supports.    |
| **3. Scale Galleries**      | Scattered records, damaged indexing stations, first signs that the Archive reorganizes itself.    |
| **4. Cinder Territory**     | Patrols of hatchlings, Arena shrines, possible negotiation with Cindara.                          |
| **5. Arachnidrake Warrens** | Tribal territory, ritual fire pits, scholars who know pieces of the Guardian succession.          |
| **6. The Archive**          | Most knowledge scales, damaged catalog minds, contradictory records from alternate possibilities. |
| **7. Webbed Threshold**     | Silk becomes architecture. The Swarm forms faces, doors, and warnings.                            |
| **8. The Bridge**           | Temporal matrix, fire well, Web of Divinity, and the current Guardian.                            |

# 8. Encounters and Complications

**The Face in the Wall:** The Swarm forms a familiar face using stolen memories, then asks the party to leave.

**A Scale That Hasn’t Happened:** A record depicts the party causing a disaster later in the expedition.

**Cindara’s Price:** She will reveal a safe path only in exchange for Arena content, a worthy opponent, or news of the outside.

**The Guardian’s Fracture:** Kek speaks in two voices: his own and the darkness pressing through the Web.

**The False Archive:** A temporal echo contains pristine records but belongs to a reality where one PC died years ago.

**Silk Bulkhead:** The Swarm is holding a ruptured deck together. Burning it opens the route and kills everyone below.

**The Ten-Thousand-Year Map:** The party finds a Catalyst map whose old data is still strategically priceless.

**The Watching Shadow:** After using Gaze of Divinity, a PC notices an observer that no one else can perceive.

## A Simple Web Corruption Track (GM Adaptation)

| **Stage**           | **Effect**                                                                     |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **0 — Unexposed**   | No effect.                                                                     |
| **1 — Observed**    | The character feels watched; sleep becomes uneasy.                             |
| **2 — Overlapping** | Briefly sees alternate versions of people and rooms.                           |
| **3 — Certain**     | Gains a useful answer but becomes convinced one possibility must occur.        |
| **4 — Fractured**   | Memories from another timeline overwrite personal memories.                    |
| **5 — Open**        | The darkness can communicate through the character.                            |
| **6 — Claimed**     | The character becomes a conduit, Guardian candidate, or campaign-level threat. |

# 9. Adventure Hooks
- The Confederacy hires the party to recover one specific scale from the Archive before a rival relic hunter finds it.
- Kek’s last message indicates that the Guardian mantle is failing and names one of the PCs as a possible successor.
- A recovered scale proves that a supposedly dead god survived—but the scale comes from a discarded timeline.
- Cindara wants an Arena expansion that exists only on a sealed world or aboard another Great Ship.
- The Swarm begins broadcasting a distress signal using the voices of previous explorers.
- A modern Catalyst has vanished. The only surviving coordinates are in the Flame’s ten-thousand-year-old registry.
- Someone has removed a black scale from the Archive, and the darkness aboard the ship is no longer contained.
- The party must use the Web to avert a war, knowing that every possible peace requires a different personal sacrifice.

# 10. Canon Boundaries for the GM

## Firmly Established
- The Flame of Knowledge is a fire-aspected Great Ship in the Vagrant Fleet’s remains.
- The Web of Divinity is linked to the bridge’s temporal matrix and can inspect possibilities.
- Prolonged contact causes insanity and risks spreading a trapped darkness.
- The Swarm is a shared intelligence of immense numbers of spiders and guards the bridge.
- The Archive contains most of the ship’s knowledge scales.
- Knowledge scales include ancient maps, schematics, Catalyst data, and battle records.
- Arachnidrakes, Cindara’s hatchlings, Kek, fire silk, chitin shards, and Gaze of Divinity are canonical.

## Not Publicly Specified
- The exact number of knowledge scales.
- A formal color or subject taxonomy for the scales.
- A complete deck plan or full list of ship levels.
- The Web’s exact visual design and complete operating rules.
- Kek’s present status after taking the Guardian mantle.
- The precise identity and nature of the trapped darkness.

## Added in This Guide

The color-coded archive categories, eight-zone expedition structure, connection procedure, corruption track, encounter seeds, and many sensory details are original GM adaptations. They are designed to fit the published lore but should not be mistaken for quotations or official mechanics.

# 11. Read-Aloud Descriptions

## Entering the Archive

The corridor widens into a vault too large for your lights to measure. Shelves climb away into darkness, not stacked with books but with scales—red, bronze, ivory, and colors that change when you look away. Some are no larger than a fingernail. Others are broad enough to serve as shields. Thin lines of fire move across their surfaces like writing beneath skin. Every few seconds one scale lifts, rotates toward an unseen listener, and settles somewhere else in the endless dark.

## Approaching the Webbed Bridge

The first strand is as thick as a cable. The next is wider than your arm. Beyond it, the passage disappears beneath layers of silk and moving black bodies. The walls seem to breathe. A ripple passes through the spiders, and millions of legs stop at once. The silence that follows is worse than the skittering. Then the mass folds inward, shaping eyes, a mouth, and a face that was never alive.

## The Web of Divinity

The bridge hangs above a well of white fire. Suspended over it is a web that cannot decide how far away it is. Some strands vanish into pinpoints of light; others pass through the walls or divide into a thousand translucent copies. Each intersection contains a scene: a battle never fought, a child never born, a world that still lives, a friend turning to speak words you have not yet heard. The temporal matrix wakes, and every possibility turns toward you.
