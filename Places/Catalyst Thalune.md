# Thalune

The planet is mostly ocean. One vast, dark sea under a sky choked with storm-clouds that never break. The water is black from orbit — not polluted, just deep beyond reason. Sonar says the ocean floor is ninety kilometers down in places. That's not geology. That's divinity.

Thalune is at the bottom. Or rather, Thalune *is* the bottom.

The god's body lies face-down on what used to be a sea floor before the weight of divine death pressed it into a trench. Over millennia the ocean filled the wound. What remains is a submerged humanoid skeleton of impossible scale, its arms outstretched, its ribcage collapsed inward, its skull half-buried in silt that glows faintly blue where bioluminescent organisms feed on residual water-magic. The bones are smooth, dark, and dense — closer to coral than calcium, crusted with deep-sea growth that pulses with tidal rhythm even this far from any moon.

Getting down there is the problem. The pressure at depth is lethal to conventional hulls. The water itself resists intrusion — currents that shouldn't exist at those depths push back against descending vessels like a living immune response. And the things that live in the trench are old, patient, and hungry.

3 - How did this god or goddess die?
Thalune was a lesser sea-god — a deep-current shepherd who guided ocean worlds through tidal crises and kept their seas from boiling off during stellar flares. During a war between greater gods, a stray death-curse meant for someone else struck Thalune mid-dive. The curse was a drowning hex of enormous power, and the irony was not lost on anyone: a sea-god, drowned in its own domain. Thalune's body sank to the deepest point it could find and its divine weight pressed the sea floor into a trench. The ocean rushed in to fill the wound, and the god's residual magic bonded with the water permanently. The sea itself became the memorial.

4 - What magical materials were created?

Tideite: dark, coral-like bone material harvested from Thalune's skeleton. It's naturally pressure-resistant and resonates with water magic. Used for deep-dive hull plating, pressure-resistant weapon housings, and aquatic construct cores. Equipment made with tideite is nearly impossible to crush.

Abyssal Pearl: small luminous spheres found in clusters around the skull and ribcage, formed where divine water-magic condensed around grains of silt over millennia. Each pearl holds a single charge of water magic that can be released on contact — used as spell-ammunition, emergency breathing sources (creates a bubble of breathable air), or trade goods of significant value.

Silt-veil gauze: a fine, semi-transparent membrane that forms naturally where bioluminescent organisms weave themselves into sheets across the skeleton. It filters toxins from any liquid passed through it and glows faintly in the presence of poison. Used in water purification systems, medical filters, and alchemical distillation.

Pressure Ink: a dense black liquid that pools in Thalune's collapsed ribcage. It's water that has been saturated with so much divine pressure-magic that it behaves more like liquid metal. Used to inscribe water-wards, pressure-seal ship hulls, and as ammunition for hydraulic weapons. A vial thrown hard enough acts like a shaped charge.

5 - Does the Catalyst have a Guardian?

Yes — **The Drowned Shepherd**.

    Form and nature: a massive deep-sea leviathan — eyeless, pale, and serpentine, with a body covered in bioluminescent gill-fronds that trail behind it like a wedding veil. It has been feeding on Thalune's residual magic for so long that it has become partially divine itself. It moves through the trench silently despite its size, and it can control local currents the way a conductor controls an orchestra.
    Role and temperament: watchful and melancholy. It does not attack on sight — it herds. It redirects intruding vessels away from the skeleton using current manipulation and pressure walls. If herding fails, it escalates to direct contact: wrapping around hulls and slowly increasing pressure until they crack or retreat. It seems to mourn Thalune and treats the skeleton as a shrine.
    Strength: appropriate for a Divinity 5 Catalyst — powerful in its environment but not invincible. It can be outmaneuvered by fast vessels or driven off by sustained fire, though killing it is harder — it regenerates in water.
    Hook: the Shepherd responds to offerings. Dumping fresh water (not recycled) or living aquatic organisms into the trench seems to calm it. Some scholars believe it is lonely.

6 - What are the dominant primal types?

    Primary: Water-primals — pressure-elementals, current-wraiths, and brine-spirits that flow through the trench like blood through veins. They attack by manipulating water pressure, creating localized whirlpools, and flooding sealed compartments.
    Secondary: Deep-primals — bioluminescent predators, abyssal crawlers, and silt-phantoms adapted to extreme depth. They rely on ambush, darkness, and patience. Many can douse light sources.
    Tertiary/associated types: Coral-constructs — semi-living growths of tideite-infused coral that form barriers, traps, and slow-moving guardians around the skeleton. They grow to seal breaches and can entomb intruders in mineral casing over hours.
