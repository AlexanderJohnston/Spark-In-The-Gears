# Session 27 Design

**Give the crew a local Maena problem they can change, with Yraina's family history and the memory device at its center.**

The immediate opportunity is to obtain a reliable way to restore the infiltrators before Yraina takes them into Maena. That can support an entire adventure or become a short acquisition before the deeper operation. Prepare the people, assets, competing interests, and consequences at both levels; let the players determine the weight.

This design starts from [[Session 26 Scratchpad]]. Everything newly proposed below is GM design material, not an event that has already happened.

## Working assumptions

- **Device owner:** Kyrsa Maena, Yraina's mother, is the working interpretation of “my old Matriarch.” The logs establish their relationship but do not explicitly identify the owner in Session 26. If these are different women, keep the personal authority and device ownership separate.
- **Geography:** give Yraina a lead to a **Maena household facility on the Trade Moon's surface**. This is a proposed location, chosen to support the local adventure the table anticipated. Kyrsa can own the facility without being physically present.
- **Scope:** both the device acquisition and the Maena infiltration remain available. There is no requirement to complete both next session.
- **Tone:** capable people taking an audacious risk against familiar kinds of power. The cosmic stakes remain real, but the immediate problems have people, property, habits, and leverage the PCs can reach.

## The strongest design material

### A family that keeps the right to give you yourself back

**Proposed institution: a private Maena memory repository and restoration service.**

It holds the device alongside records of people whose memories the household considers valuable. Some are specialists kept useful through repeated assignments; some are dependents whose restoration has become a reward; some are family records whose custody gives Kyrsa leverage over her own circle.

This provides a concrete expression of Maena's power. The family preserves people selectively because retaining the ability to restore them is profitable and controlling.

The device can work exactly as Yraina hopes. The pressure comes from **who controls access to it, what else is stored with it, and what taking it changes**.

A useful reason for its present location is routine servicing or a household deployment connected to the moon's expanded operations. Choose one. This creates a reachable asset without requiring the players to raid the family seat on Inura or confront its entire leadership.

The facility's vulnerability follows from normal work: equipment needs maintenance, specialists need access, records change custody, and people have incompatible interests. These are opportunities the players can discover and exploit through their own methods.

### Yraina knows the culture, but has been away from its power

Her fifteen years on the surface give her a local life to draw on; her estrangement gives her a personal history with the household. Neither supplies universal current clearance.

Give her **one enduring relationship and one obsolete assumption**:

- A former household specialist remembers her treating them as a person and will hear her out.
- A procedure she once relied upon now answers to a different authority.

These are suggested ingredients. The obsolete assumption should produce discoverable uncertainty, not a compulsory betrayal or an unavoidable capture.

Her most interesting temptation is to perform the old role because it works: the aristocrat who expects compliance, the trader who prices other people, the daughter who knows which kind of cruelty gets a quick result. Let her be effective and let Asha decide what to make of it. A scene of confession is unnecessary.

## People with reasons to move

Use the roles that become relevant. They need not all appear.

| Person or interest | What they want | What they can offer or obstruct | A productive contradiction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Yraina** | A real strike against Maena, and recognition as someone who can make it happen | Family knowledge, social entry, the wipe, and the intended restoration | Her most effective skills were learned inside the system she hates. |
| **Kyrsa / the owning Matriarch** | Control of household assets, reputation, and useful people | Legitimate access, threats of dispossession, a bargain that makes theft unnecessary | She may genuinely value Yraina while understanding that relationship in terms of ownership. Any concealed binding intensifies particular priorities; it need not explain ordinary family cruelty. |
| **The memory custodian** *(new role)* | Keep the repository intact and avoid becoming the person blamed for losing it | Technical knowledge, equipment access, custody records, a credible account of what restoration needs | Preserving people's memories can feel humane to someone who still denies those people the right to use them. |
| **A repeatedly wiped specialist** *(new role)* | Recover a particular part of their past, leave, or secure restoration for someone else | Knowledge of what happens between intake and assignment; access earned through work | They may know the household's routines better than Yraina while knowing less about their own life. Their help has terms of their choosing. |
| **A local Maena administrator** *(new role)* | Keep operations profitable and contain anything that could invite senior scrutiny | Labor transfers, supply routes, security, and records | A quiet, deniable loss may be preferable to a public demonstration of disloyalty or incompetence. |

A local administrator can be bought, deceived, intimidated, bypassed, or killed. Decide what authority survives them rather than granting them immunity.

The hidden Necrotis follower remains a deeper interest. It need not appear, speak, or detect the PCs this session. Its existence can be expressed through an authorization nobody present can countermand.

## Assets worth fighting or bargaining over

| Asset | Immediate use | Further use if the players pursue it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The imprinting device, with usable restoration capability** | Makes Yraina's preferred infiltration credible | Repeated covert work or restoring people Maena keeps dependent |
| **A reliable pre-wipe imprint and knowledge of its use** | A known way back for each volunteer | Evidence of how the household distinguishes a useful mind from a disposable identity |
| **Labor placement authority or an authenticated destination** | Directs the infiltration somewhere worth reaching | Connects the household to a Spark-related facility or intermediary |
| **A specialist's cooperation** | Makes the equipment usable and the cover informed | An insider relationship that persists after the current job |
| **An older household record** | Exposes a change in policy or custody | A comparison point for the Matriarch's unusual decisions, if that deeper thread becomes relevant |

Possession of the machine should deliver a real gain. If an operator, reference, or restoration component is necessary, make that a legible part of the asset before the crew commits to taking it. Avoid revealing after a successful theft that they have stolen a useless box and need another unrelated expedition.

Other stored memories are optional stakes. Choose one meaningful example rather than presenting an archive of tragic biographies the crew must process before acting.

The crew can take only what it came for. Helping others can produce allies and obligations without becoming a moral test that invalidates the main objective.

## Several approaches, with different consequences

These are ways the material supports player invention, not a menu to deliver through an NPC.

| Approach | Why it could work | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Take the device quietly** | The facility must move equipment and admit people who service it | The household investigates a loss; attribution depends on actual evidence |
| **Use Yraina's standing** | A family relationship can secure attention ordinary money cannot | Access may be framed as a return to obligations she believed she had escaped |
| **Bargain with its keeper or owner** | A specialist, administrator, or Matriarch may value something else more urgently | The crew gains a usable asset while someone acquires a claim, secret, favor, or advantage |
| **Turn the household's dependents into allies** | People under its control possess knowledge and reasons to resist | The operation gains participants whose goals extend beyond the crew's original mission |
| **Seize the relevant assets by force** | The crew's new equipment and Kaelith's power should matter at this scale | Local resistance can be beaten; witnesses and visible damage give Maena better information |
| **Change the entry plan** | Yraina explicitly said she had alternatives; Kaelith raised an Inuran disguise | The crew may pursue a visitor, contractor, delivery, or compromised-insider route, trading the wipe risk for different limits on access |

None requires Kyrsa's death. None prevents the players deciding she should die. If they attempt it, her location, protection, and the succession problem determine the consequences.

## The memory proposal needs understandable boundaries

The interesting risk is trusting a person and a recovery arrangement. It becomes difficult to judge if “wipe” can mean anything the GM needs at the moment.

**Recommended working model, to establish before anyone undergoes the procedure:**

| Question | Proposed answer | Design value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What disappears? | Access to autobiographical memory and mission knowledge. Language and practiced skills remain. | Wiped characters can function, notice, act, and make choices. The players still have characters to play. |
| What does the imprint do? | Preserves the individual reference needed for this method to reopen access. | The procedure has a concrete dependency without declaring that the device contains a removable soul. |
| What proves the method is usable? | Inspection, an existing restoration case, or a small voluntary demonstration can establish its actual behavior. | Trust can be informed. A stranger's confident promise need not carry the whole plan. |
| What is required to restore someone? | The correct imprint, compatible equipment, access to the subject, and someone able to operate it. | Custody and recovery remain meaningful logistical choices. Yraina should be able to explain what her proposal requires. |
| Does wiping make someone obedient? | No. Vulnerability and lack of context make coercion easier; obedience requires additional methods or circumstances. | The crew enters a system that exploits confusion, and the PCs retain the ability to resist or improvise. |
| Are memories made during the wipe lost? | They remain unless a separate, known procedure removes them. | Restoration adds the missing history back; it does not annul the experiences or choices made inside. |

These are proposed campaign rules, not claims established by the Session 26 dialogue.

Use the same principles consistently for NPCs and PCs. Restoring autobiography does not automatically remove a Spirit binding, and the mixed divine binding on the Matriarch is not just an elaborate version of a routine wipe. Revival brew addresses recent bodily death under Voria's stated conditions; it is not a promised solution to either problem.

Axiom can help inspect boundaries or recognize that something has been concealed. It is not yet a full Spirit diagnostician or a substitute memory archive. Its own experience is distinct from Kaelith's; whether it retains impressions is worth defining, but it cannot supply a fluent briefing or automatic restoration.

The living armor relationships deserve the same clarity. Decide what an armor partner would remember, how it communicates, and whether entering with it fits the intended cover. Do not spring an unannounced wipe of a separate intelligence, or an automatic immunity that makes the whole premise irrelevant.

### The players still own participation

Asha's yes does not enroll every companion. The next substantive question is who takes which risk.

- **If both PCs are wiped:** give the players control of their immediate choices, perceptions, and relationships. Confusion can be brief or central according to their interest; it need not become a prolonged guessing game about facts the players already know.
- **If one remains aware:** that PC can participate through a different status or support role. Build active work for both; avoid making one player watch an NPC escort the other through the adventure.
- **If neither is wiped:** the device can still be leverage, a means to help someone, or unnecessary to a different plan.
- **Nyrix and Bell:** their inclusion remains a choice. Staying close, preserving recovery capability, and entering under the slave cover are separate roles.

A short agreement about recovery custody and authority can do the necessary work. The bridge need not hold another lengthy council.

## What getting inside Maena is for

**Recommended operational target: establish how Maena places controlled labor into its concealed work, and secure access to one useful destination.**

This follows directly from Yraina's premise that the organization relies on slaves. The PCs need not reach the Umbral mine or the command center of the entire program to gain useful leverage.

Choose one reachable destination behind that labor system:

| Candidate target | What the crew could gain | What makes entry consequential |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A labor allocation office with restricted assignments** | A real facility, intermediary, or transfer chain to investigate | They can redirect who gets sent where, including themselves and potential allies |
| **A neural-interface testing annex** | A concrete link back to the lattice Maena blocked from Axiom's purchase | Kaelith can examine how magic, memory, and control are being applied without needing to solve Spark's origin |
| **A reserve diagnostics contractor** | Evidence that Maena is studying why stored Spark will not behave as expected | The crew can interfere with a research dependency while Rho investigates the physical reserve |
| **A household authorization archive** | Proof that an external instruction can override a commercially sensible Matriarchal decision | The hidden operator becomes an investigable relationship, rather than a sudden new villain reveal |

Only one needs development for the chosen approach. The others can remain possibilities.

The wiped-slave cover should solve a particular access problem: it places apparently disoriented labor inside a system closed to hostile outsiders. It does not by itself hide famous faces, justify carrying artifacts, choose the right assignment, or guarantee freedom of movement.

Those are useful problems for the crew to solve. Their fame should matter where someone has reason and opportunity to recognize them; it should not make every employee omniscient. Yraina's claim that acting cannot reproduce a wipe is her confidence in her favored method, not a universal prohibition on clever deception.

## A restrained connection to the bound Matriarch

The best early clue is **a decision that does not fit the family's own interests**.

Possible forms:

- A profitable specialist is withheld from work because a separate office insists on custody.
- A restoration normally available to valued family members has been suspended for a narrow, unexplained category.
- A loss the Matriarch would once have punished is repeatedly accepted when the same hidden authority is involved.

Use one if the chosen location can naturally provide it. An old policy, an employee's experience, or a household record can establish the contrast. Yraina can recognize something as personally unusual without diagnosing its magical cause.

The clue earns a useful question: **who can make Maena act against its own advantage?**

It does not prove the mixed-blood binding, identify the Necrotis follower, or exonerate Kyrsa. If the device owner is a different matriarch, put this clue in the Maena operation itself rather than attaching the binding to the wrong person.

## Let delegation work

Asha asked for progress in several places. Honor that by letting each accepted assignment achieve something proportional to its people, access, and time.

| Assignment | Useful independent work | A gain worth bringing back | Boundary or decision it may need |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rho and Smoke: Saezo** | Secure a protective role and learn how the reserve is operated | A real control dependency, trusted operator, or observation point | Authority to interfere when a visible emergency threshold is crossed |
| **Seraphine and Tamsin: Ternus** | Follow procurement through contacts and institutions Seraphine understands | A contractor, authorization chain, or distinction between routine supply and exceptional transfers | Whether exposing a source or stopping a shipment is worth the effect on Saezo and on continued access |
| **Lysa: Necrotis research** | Study permitted records, samples, or systems relevant to coercion | A comparison method or specific sign that ordinary Spirit explanations are incomplete | Nara's answer and the terms of access |
| **Nara: divine politics** | Brief selected allies and argue for limited, coordinated action | Time, political cover, or a useful specialist contact | Evidence she can share without exposing the Web or accelerating a factional response |

**Recommended answer to Lysa's pending request:** limited, supervised study is possible, with the scope agreed around identifying and breaking coercion. Nara's hostility to Necrotis should shape the terms, while her new warmth toward the crew makes a constructive answer plausible. Records or a controlled sample can keep Lysa productive without forcing Kaelith into a premature catalyst trip.

That is a proposed answer, not an approval that has already arrived.

Before the teams leave, establish only what matters: transport, who keeps the Lone Star operational, how they report, and who has authority if Asha and Kaelith are deliberately unavailable. Do not assume Aran has agreed to transport everyone.

Offscreen reports work best as **something achieved, something learned, and a decision only if one is needed**. A team can simply succeed. They do not all need emergencies that recall the PCs.

An especially useful connection is for one assignment to make another easier: Seraphine identifies a contractor the PCs can impersonate; Rho finds an operating practice the PCs recognize in a Maena record; Lysa helps interpret evidence the PCs actually brought back. Let those connections arise from compatible discoveries rather than requiring every team to finish a preset part of a puzzle.

## Pressure that follows evidence

| Pressure | What makes it advance | What the players can see or influence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Local custody of the device** | A detected theft, irregular request, witness, or contested transfer | Inventory inquiries, changed access, a keeper seeking a quiet settlement |
| **Yraina's family exposure** | Someone learns she is using her relationship to move household assets or people | A private demand, a withdrawn favor, an attempt to reclaim her publicly |
| **Saezo's control** | Credible evidence of losses or rivals taking authority he believed he possessed | Consolidation of commands, restricted pump access, preparation to move reserves |
| **Maena's larger program** | Research succeeds, evidence reaches the hidden operator, or a useful dependency is disrupted | New custody rules, redirected resources, efforts to recover particular people or records |
| **Pantheon response** | Nara shares information and other powers develop interests in acting | Requests for evidence, conditions on support, diplomatic positioning |

The device theft alone does not automatically make Saezo panic or reveal the whole crew's campaign to Maena. Track what each observer can actually learn.

For Saezo's eventual dump, the earlier prep needs physical dependencies defined before that front becomes an immediate crisis. A useful proposed pair is **command authorization** and **the physical reserve-to-core feed**: falsifying or constraining orders has different risks from isolating machinery. The actual layout, safeguards, and timing remain to be chosen when Rho gains access.

## Outcomes worth having material for

- **A working way back:** the crew possesses the device and the knowledge needed to use it. They can decide how far to proceed.
- **Access with obligations:** a bargain preserves deniability but gives someone a claim the crew must later honor, evade, or break.
- **A changed relationship:** Yraina wins trust through a costly choice, or the crew learns a limit she is willing to state plainly.
- **Useful exposure:** a covert attempt becomes public, but the PCs keep an asset, ally, destination, or proof and can choose what to do with their new visibility.
- **A foothold inside:** the crew reaches a specific Maena function and gains practical authority or intelligence there.
- **A different plan:** the players reject the wipe with enough knowledge to pursue another credible entry.

These can occur together, separately, or in another form entirely. None is an assigned ending.

The central payoff is that Session 26's decisions become effective: Yraina's connection yields access, the officers earn their independence, and Asha and Kaelith can hurt a part of Maena's operation they now understand.

## Remaining GM choices

Two choices remain open: whether the device's owner is Kyrsa/the previously described Maena Matriarch, and whether acquisition or infiltration should carry more weight. The working assumptions above support either answer.

The few additional choices that make this material ready for improvisation are:

- What does the device's present keeper value enough to trade or take a risk?
- What is the device's ordinary purpose, and which restoration requirements can the crew establish?
- What one Maena function would the accepted cover realistically reach?
- What useful result can the first deployed crew team achieve while the PCs act?

Names, floor plans, exact defenses, conversations, and the order in which any of this is encountered can follow the players' approach.
