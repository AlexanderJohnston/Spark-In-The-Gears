The Skull of Xal fills them too fast, too close, a colossal bleached ruin hanging in the void like a moon flensed down to meaning. Ram-like horns curl up from the brow and hook down beneath the jaw in vast black arcs, big enough to swallow fleets into their shadows. Thick ridges of dark bone run along the crown, and from the empty eye sockets and the open mouth pours that hellish violet light: not warm, not fiery, but cold enough to make the bridge feel smaller just by looking at it. Off either side drift two artifact-class dreadnoughts, the Word of Xal and the Flame of Knowledge, parked like continents in attendance. Their hulls are still, patient, and enormous enough to make your own battleship feel like something borrowed.

  Narrator: Then the surface resolves. What looked at first like bare bone is crowded with life and industry. The skull is not dead space; it is inhabited, layered, built upon in dense, organic sprawl instead of clean city blocks. Stone-colored fields and bleached terraces cling to the curves of the bone. Fortified ridgelines, gun emplacements, and heavy spell-cannon silhouettes break up the horizon. Traffic crawls along ledges and causeways: demons large and small, winged shapes crossing open void, armored figures moving in ordered streams, clusters of bodies gathering around platforms and towers worked straight into the skull itself. Far below, marked cleanly and almost absurdly politely amid all that menace, a landing lane is waiting for you. The whole thing feels less like a station and more like arriving at the front gate of an ancient god’s court — busy, armed, and entirely confident that if you are here, it is because you were allowed to be.
