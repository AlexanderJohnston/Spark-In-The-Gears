# Asha Xal Vision

**Narrator: Asha:** The universe turns over.

Not metaphorically. Not emotionally. It simply **rotates**, all at once, as if reality itself has been flipped in a giant unseen hand, and then comes the second sensation: something icy, smooth, and bottomless, like falling backward into a pool made of winter and memory. The bridge, the Skull, her body - all of it vanishes. Perspective tears free from anything human. She is elsewhere and elsewhen. She exists in countless places at once, her awareness stretching across galaxies as easily as breath crossing a room. She sees in spectrums her mortal eyes were never meant to hold. She sees futures branching and dying. She sees probabilities colliding. She is not standing within the memory of a god. For one impossible span, she is the god.

She is Xal.

The scale of him is nauseating. Demonic claws large enough to prise apart a continent. Wings broad enough to cast a planet into shadow. Whatever species he had once risen from is long buried beneath eternity, though some faint geometry remains: four limbs, two eyes, the ghost of mortality stretched into divinity beyond recognition. Millions of years pass around him like weather. The dark between galaxies is not empty to him. It is textured, meaningful, full of currents and absences. When he speaks, his voice does not travel through air. It arrives directly in the mind of its intended target, vast and cold and perfectly clear.

**"What have you brought me to see, sorcerer? Speak, Talifax."**

Below him - or ahead of him, or merely smaller within this impossible frame of reference - stands Talifax in dark armor, head bowed, hands clasped before him in a formal standing bow.

**"Thank you for blessing me with your presence, great Xal,"** Talifax says. **"My mistress will arrive in a moment. I beg your indulgence. Please, only for a moment."**

**"Do not grovel, mortal,"** Xal replies.

Then something moves through the void.

At first Asha mistakes it for missing starlight. Then the shape resolves: a dragon, midnight-scaled, swimming through open space as if the vacuum were deep water. Her eyes are pools of pure void magic, so dark they border on absence. She blends against the stars best where there are no stars at all. Nefarius.

**"I have called you here because both of us are in danger,"** she says.

Xal's attention narrows toward a region of the night sky that seems, at first glance, unremarkable. It is merely empty. A sparse stretch. A place where light has thinned. But that is only how it looks to lesser sight.

**"For many millennia this region has quietly grown and none of us have intervened,"** Nefarius says.

The emptiness spreads subtly across the galactic map. Strange, but not alarming. Almost natural. Galaxies drift through space in irregular patterns all the time. Xal considers it, and Asha can feel the terrible scale of that consideration - not seconds of thought, but the testing of models vast enough to swallow civilizations.

**"This appears to be a natural phenomenon,"** Xal says at last. **"Galaxies spin through space. What happened here is a haphazard pattern and nothing more."**

**"I do not believe that to be the case,"** Nefarius answers. **"This phenomenon is meant to look natural, but is anything but."**

The name arrives with recognition: **Botee's Void**. A region many millions of light-years wide, where almost no stars remain. The few galaxies that survive there are isolated islands in overwhelming dark, their night skies nearly black. It feels, even to Asha's mortal memory buried somewhere deep inside this vision, disturbingly like the Umbral Depths.

Xal's perception reaches into it.

He scans the void in widening layers, through stellar drift and ancient dust and the cold mathematics of absence, until he finds the center. And there - there - is something wrong in a way that no mortal word can hold. A dark speck, impossibly small against galactic scale, yet absolute. Light that touches it does not reflect. Magic that touches it does not ripple. Both simply vanish. Worse: Xal cannot perceive what lies within it. For the first time in the vision, Asha feels surprise from him. Real surprise. Sharp and immediate.

**"A tear in our plane,"** Xal says.

**"That's right,"** Nefarius replies. **"Cleverly hidden and so slow that it will take billions of years to swallow this plane. The pantheon might have been blind to it if not for my discovery."**

Xal's thoughts shift. Not in words, but in chains of consequence unfolding so fast they almost become instinct. Asha feels him testing futures - thousands, millions, then trillions - and in nearly all of them there is accusation. Fear. War. She sees a blur of gods turning on one another. She sees herself - or rather Xal - surrounded, assailed, destroyed by a united pantheon before the details can even resolve.

**"Why tell only me?"** Xal asks. **"Why not the entire pantheon?"**

Nefarius does not hesitate.

**"Of all the pantheon, you and I are the only gods whose magic is primarily void. Our magic is, in a way, linked to the Umbral Depths, and they all know it. The ancient prohibitions prevent the denizens from working such magics. They are trapped. That means this phenomenon must have been created by a god or goddess on our plane. Someone has betrayed us, Xal, and we will be blamed."**

The future flexes.

Asha feels Xal run possibilities so quickly they become pain. In every line worth naming, mistrust sharpens into certainty. The pantheon sees the dark magic, sees the void linkage, sees the tear, and lays the blame where it has always been easiest to lay it: on the gods they never fully trusted.

Then Xal stills.

He returns from the storm of futures with terrifying calm.

**"Yes,"** Xal says. **"I begin to see. They will blame us for this."**

Nefarius drifts there in the darkness, vast and patient and unreadable.

**"They already mistrust us as it is, simply because our magic is darker than theirs,"** Xal says. **"We will study this tear and then bring our findings to the pantheon - but only when we have a way to repair it."**

**"Of course, great Xal,"** Nefarius says.

And that answer lands wrong.

Not loudly. Not enough to break the scene. Just enough for something inside Asha to recoil as the vision holds for one breath too long. Xal accepts the exchange. The memory continues. But buried within his vastness, within all that power and age and sight, she feels the first cold edge of a truth that comes too late:

He has already seen his death waiting at the far end of this path.
