# Session 21 Scene Summary

## Scene 1 - Bridge restart and the Absorb / Release retrofit

### What happened

The session resumed on the freighter bridge immediately after Seraphine pulled the auxiliary data core free. The GM re-offered the investigation menu, but the group stayed local and kept searching the ship. Asha started by looking more closely at the captain's console and found an Inuran retrofit with two additional controls labeled **Absorb** and **Release**.

Rho identified it as some kind of weapon modification. Seraphine recognized it with visible unease and explained that Ternus had used ships infused with **Nefarius's blood** during the war. Those ships could consume magic in front of them and then fire freely into mage-heavy opposition. She also explained that the wider program ultimately helped awaken Nefarius when Ternus consumed a god corpse to fuel the effort.

Asha was revolted by the whole thing and especially by how matter-of-fact Seraphine could sound while describing it.

### Major points

- Seraphine still has the auxiliary data core from the bridge.
- The freighter or its class was tied to a war-era anti-magic weapon system.
- The labels **Absorb** and **Release** are now a direct bridge-control clue, not just vague atmosphere.
- The hull is tied to Nefarius through infused blood.
- Asha's disgust toward Ternus and toward Seraphine's partial rationalization intensified.

### GM needs to remember

- Seraphine's reaction matters. This is war memory, not abstract history, for her.
- The weaponized Nefarius-blood ships are now explicit in play.
- The bridge console should stay relevant for later technical interpretation.
- The session's investigation menu is still a good fallback if players stall next time.

### Players need to remember

- The ship was not just a cargo vessel; it was tied to anti-magic wartime doctrine.
- **Absorb / Release** is a concrete clue worth revisiting with Systems, Engineering, or lore.
- Seraphine has the aux core and expects it to matter later.

## Scene 2 - Cargo-bay Essence Sight and the hull's Nefarius taint

### What happened

Kaelith activated Essence Sight and moved into the cargo bays while Asha and the others remained focused on the bridge for a moment. He successfully resisted being overwhelmed and began reading the ship in layers rather than just surfaces. The cargo spaces appeared as stacked magical history: wards, old handling residue, air sigils, life sigils, and the marks of purposeful work rather than random storage.

He found evidence that something important had once sat in the bays and had later been removed in a hurry. The residue did not yield a clean answer, but it clearly pointed to deliberate special handling.

At the same time, Asha asked her armor whether it sensed Nefarius here. The armor confirmed that Seraphine's description fit what it felt: the freighter was indeed touched by Nefarius, and specifically by **her blood in the hull itself**. The armor also implied it could probably devour that blood later if Asha wished.

### Major points

- Kaelith saw the cargo deck as layered magical history rather than normal ship space.
- The cargo bays held something important, handled carefully, and later removed fast.
- Life sigils and air sigils suggest mage or god-linked cargo handling.
- Asha's armor confirmed Nefarius blood is in the hull itself.
- The armor likely can consume that blood later, though doing so may be dramatic.

### GM needs to remember

- Do not let Nefarius become the whole answer. In Session 20 the armor already framed Nefarius as a coating over something worse.
- The cargo spaces should continue to feel like a site of special transport, not routine shipping.
- If Asha chooses to let the armor devour the hull-taint later, that should feel significant and visible.

### Players need to remember

- The cargo bays held something special, dangerous, or sacred.
- Nefarius is physically present in the freighter's hull.
- Kaelith can keep getting answers from the ship by reading magical residue carefully.

## Scene 3 - Cargo-bay flame read and the spirit massacre

### What happened

Kaelith followed up his Essence Sight with a successful flame read in the cargo bay. He rolled back through the ship's history, past recent emptiness, past the party's own earlier salvage, and all the way to the ship's active wartime period roughly thirty years earlier.

He saw ordinary loading procedures at first. Then a tightly handled special shipment arrived aboard under the supervision of strange Inurans dressed in distinctive **blue-and-white** outfits unlike the normal crew and workers. Kaelith recognized the cargo as the party's armor.

Almost immediately afterward, spirits emerged through the walls and slaughtered the crew in seconds. Cleanup followed, bodies were removed, and the ship was put on autopilot for its final destination.

### Major points

- The armor was loaded aboard intentionally under special supervision.
- The supervising Inurans wore a distinct blue-and-white style later associated with Maena.
- Spirit magic was involved from the beginning of the freighter's disaster, not just at the dock attack.
- The original crew was wiped out almost instantly.
- The ship was deliberately sent onward after the massacre.

### GM needs to remember

- Kaelith specifically asked whether people emerged from the suits; the answer was no, the armor was carried aboard as cargo.
- The armor's presence on the ship is not random salvage luck anymore; it is part of the larger plot.
- The spirit attack should tie forward into the masked spirit binder and Maena suspicion.

### Players need to remember

- The armor shipment was planned, important, and connected to the ship's fall.
- Spirits killed the original crew.
- The people who brought the armor aboard are now one of the strongest leads in the mystery.

## Scene 4 - Officers' quarters and the masked spirit binder

### What happened

Once the group decided the bridge had yielded what it could for the moment, they moved to the officers' section. On the way, Asha mentioned that her own brief Essence Sight on the bridge had shown ancient-looking spellscript, and she received **+1 EXP** after reflecting that she actually knows more about magic than she tends to admit.

The officer quarters were cleaner and more personal than the cargo spaces, but less interesting on the surface. Kaelith then flame read the captain's quarters and saw something decisive: the captain had already been compromised before the voyage. A **spirit binder**, masked behind heavy concealment, was guiding or controlling the captain's actions before the ship ever left Inuran port.

Kaelith called everyone over, summarized both the cargo-bay massacre and the new captain's-quarters revelation, and explicitly emphasized the spirit attack that followed the armor delivery.

### Major points

- Asha got **+1 EXP** during this session.
- The captain was not acting freely; he was under a spirit binder's control before launch.
- The freighter's fate was engineered before departure, not just improvised during transit.
- The officer section reinforces that ordinary crew life once existed here before the ship became horror and evidence.

### GM needs to remember

- The binder was hidden well enough that even a strong flame read did not reveal a clean identity.
- The captain's lack of agency matters morally and politically.
- Asha's growth in magical confidence is now text, not subtext.

### Players need to remember

- Someone took control of the captain before the voyage began.
- The freighter's fall was premeditated.
- Asha is improving and should not keep downplaying her own magical competence.

## Scene 5 - Yraina names Maena, and Lysa proposes speaking with the dead

### What happened

When Kaelith described the strange uniforms and spirit-magic methods, Yraina immediately connected the dots. She said the description sounded like **Maena**, an unusual Inuran family known for embracing spirit magic and for producing spell armor. Asha reacted with renewed disgust, and Kaelith asked whether that meant Maena also coveted Nefarius. Yraina did not know the full motive, only that Maena clearly seemed to be "wielding the knife."

Lysa then entered the magical analysis more directly. At Kaelith's urging, she performed a brief flame read despite hating the headaches it causes her. She confirmed that the spirit binder had masked himself well, cursed binders in general, and then proposed a stronger route: if the crew could get **Joren's body**, and if Joren and the Inuran captain had not fully moved on, she believed she could try to get them to talk. She explained that even if two dead people do not end up in the same spiritual place, dying in the same ship leaves them close enough for a skilled spirit worker to try something.

The session ended with Asha realizing that Ternus likely already has Joren's body.

### Major points

- Yraina named **Maena** directly as the strongest live suspect.
- Maena is associated with spirit magic and spell armor, not just cybernetics and trade.
- Lysa confirmed the binder used strong masking.
- Joren's body is now a major investigative objective.
- Spirit testimony is on the table for the first time.

### GM needs to remember

- Yraina sounded informed and annoyed, not surprised.
- Lysa hates flame reading but will push herself when the answer matters.
- The next session should answer where Joren's body is, who controls it, or what it will cost to reach it.
- The ancient layer is still unresolved even with Maena named; do not let the mystery flatten.

### Players need to remember

- Maena is now the strongest present-day suspect.
- Lysa may be able to get testimony from Joren and possibly the captain.
- Ternus custody over Joren's body is likely the next practical obstacle.
