# Maena Raid GM Guide

## Operation at a glance

**The party enters Maena's Trade Moon headquarters as Yraina's wiped servants, recovers its memories and equipment in household holding, and strikes the operation from inside. Kyrsa is elsewhere.**

**The primary objective is to discover why Maena wants the Spark and what it is building.** The answer is a manufactured god: neural-lattice magitech hardware that Maena intends to bring to life with Spark. This would bypass the origin shared by every existing god—a living mortal mage who grew powerful enough to become divine.

The raid must offer clear evidence of that ambition and obvious indications that Maena knows about exceptional power associated with **Terra and Sol**. Disabling the research and obstructing the takeover of Saezo's infrastructure are substantial optional gains. Restricted correspondence can also expose an offsite authority directing the program. An extraction with convincing answers is a successful operation even if the PCs leave the laboratory functioning.

This develops the approved [[Maena Raid Draft]]. The servant-and-wipe route is now selected by the GM. Names, layout, procedures, evidence, and timing introduced here are **new operational preparation**, not events that already happened. Use them consistently during play. The earlier log and subsequent GM corrections remain authoritative.

Start this guide **after resolving the Educator exit** using [[Session 28 Design]]. Do not narrate an escape, rescued specialist, copied records, or three recovered cases that the players have not actually secured.

| Scene | Purpose | Default connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Aboard the Lone Star** | Secure the gain, verify the method, establish support, and make the imprints. | The prepared group travels to headquarters and enters as servants. |
| **2. Household intake** | Let the cover obtain real access while creating a record of Yraina's return. | A disputed personal-servant declaration puts all three in household holding. |
| **3. Holding and restoration** | Return the PCs' memory access and give them the opportunity to recover gear and initiative. | Household review or the service corridor provides access to the working facility. |
| **4. Specialist allocation** | Reveal the scale of neural-lattice production, flag Sol by name, and make the internal operation readable. | Research and the director's suite become concrete targets. |
| **5. The neural-lattice laboratory** | Reveal the manufactured-god project and Spark's intended role; offer a consequential research target. | The raid can proceed to leadership or extraction with the central answer. |
| **6. The director and the protected liaison** | Establish Maena's ambition, its knowledge of Terra/Sol, the takeover mechanism, and an outside authority. | Resolve their response, then leave with whatever the party has gained. |
| **7. Extraction and consequences** | Let the chosen gains survive the raid and update the divided crew's situation. | A usable victory and a specific next lead. |

Scenes 1–3 follow the selected entry method. **Scenes 4–6 can be shortened, reordered, combined, or bypassed.** The laboratory does not become inaccessible because the PCs went to the director first. The director does not have to be defeated before they leave. The guide describes what NPCs attempt, not what the PCs must do.

Allow roughly 30–45 minutes for preparation, intake, and recovery; 60–100 minutes for the internal operation; and 20–40 minutes for extraction and consequences. These are table-time estimates, not deadlines. If the Educator takes most of a session, carry the raid forward intact.

## GM truths and boundaries

- **GM correction: Maena's objective is artificial divinity.** It manufactures neural-lattice magitech hardware and plans to use Spark to breathe life into it, creating its own magitech god. This has never been accomplished. All existing gods began as living mortal mages who grew too powerful. Maena's intended success is not an established technical possibility or an accomplished awakening.
- **GM-only Terra/Sol truth:** the extraordinary power associated with humanity's original homeworld is the most powerful Catalyst in the universe. The raid reveals Maena's knowledge of great power there, not that full explanation, a measured cosmic ranking, or a usable route to the homeworld.
- **Kyrsa Maena is Yraina's mother and the secretly bound Matriarch.** She is absent. The ancient follower of Necrotis is also offsite in this operation; its exact identity and form remain unrevealed.
- The local personnel know different pieces. No receptionist, researcher, or security officer can deliver the whole campaign explanation.
- The laboratory tests neural-lattice hardware and investigates why the affected reserve cannot animate it as intended. It has **not** solved the taint or created a god. No assumption about the purity of later Spark shipments is needed.
- This building has neither Saezo's vast reservoir nor the moon's deep Catalyst engine. Destroying its apparatus cannot remotely ignite the reserve. Saezo may react to news, but a raid alarm is not his automatic catastrophe trigger.
- The Educator machinery, Yraina's soul-linked authorization, the Hand's taint, and Kyrsa's mixed-blood binding remain separate mechanisms.
- **Yraina's failed authority remains unexplained.** Household referral below works through people and documented family identity, not a repaired invocation. The sealed soul repository is not placed in this building.
- Axiom remains at its logged Xal/Void stage. It can support appropriate examination; it cannot identify every Spirit mechanism, narrate the hidden plot, or restore the PCs by itself.

The Terra/Sol references are intentionally obvious once the relevant material is in view. Do not hide the words behind an extreme Investigation roll or encode them as an unrecognizable project number. The hard work is reaching and retaining evidence, then deciding what to do with it.

**Continuity with Session 23:** the PCs witnessed the observatory account of Sol's apparent ending, the closing of the route to Terra, and Soma Arata's role as the Hand. Yraina speculated about a Catalyst there; that was not confirmation. Modern Maena interest creates a concrete new question about what remains and what Maena knows. It does not invalidate the recording or explain how Sol's collapse, the seal, the Umbral mining, and the Catalyst fit together.

## Scene 1 — Aboard the Lone Star

**Scene goal:** the players understand what they are attempting, and the recovery arrangement works before they surrender memory access.

**Present:** Asha, Kaelith, Yraina; Nyrix as available shipboard support. Keep Lysa, Rho, Smoke, Seraphine, and Tamsin on their actual assignments.

### Establish the exit result

Record recovered kits, casualties, witnesses, copied evidence, and any rescued people. Give a rescued specialist a place to be safe and control over whether to help. He does not need to supply the next location.

Yraina identifies the headquarters and explains the household procedure she intends to use: **a family sponsor retains a declared restoration kit while a disputed personal-servant claim is referred for household review.** For this operation, her knowledge of that procedure is correct. It requires no outside assistance and grants no unrestricted clearance. Her failed soul-linked authorization remains a separate, unresolved discrepancy.

Determine whether an Educator report has reached headquarters from the witnesses, communications, and elapsed time actually established during the exit. Use the exposure adjustment in Scene 2. Yraina can assess their visible exposure but does not automatically know what headquarters has received; she should not promise that there is no alert without evidence.

### What they can know before entering

Yraina knows this headquarters houses neural-interface development, the trade office, and Kyrsa's local director. Its ordinary corporate directory identifies the laboratory's public department name, **Reserve Compatibility**, but not its hidden purpose. The party already knows Maena restricted a Spark-compatible neural lattice in Session 20 and that Kek linked Maena to a birth. These give them a concrete question to pursue. The hardware's god-making purpose and the Sol material are discoveries inside; the briefing does not leak them.

Yraina's intended immediate objective is to reach the research and executive records: what is Maena building, why does it need Spark, and what does it expect to gain? Recovering answers is the operation's priority. The rig and the takeover packet provide opportunities to act on those answers, not a compulsory demolition assignment.

### Operating rulings for the wipe and restoration

These are new rulings for this operation, made explicit before use; they are not claims that Session 27 already established them.

| Item | Ruling for play |
| --- | --- |
| **What the wipe blocks** | Access to autobiographical history and the mission briefing. Language, ordinary reasoning, trained physical and magical skills, and the capacity to make decisions remain. It does not install loyalty or obedience. |
| **The imprint** | A reference to that person's pre-wipe memory state. It is not a detachable soul or a second person. A verified duplicate can be made on another wafer. |
| **Taking an imprint** | Five uninterrupted minutes per person using a complete kit. Verification is part of the procedure. Correct routine use does not require a dramatic failure roll. |
| **Restoration** | The correct wafer, powered frame and handset, access to the subject, and one uninterrupted minute per person. Yraina can operate the kit. An interruption stops the process; it does not randomly corrupt the subject. |
| **After restoration** | Previous memories become accessible alongside memories formed since the wipe. No imposed personality change or prescribed emotional reaction. |
| **Independent beings** | Axiom and living armor remain aboard with the gear, unaffected by the PCs' wipe. They are not carried through intake as hidden backup narrators. |
| **Damage and misuse** | Physically destroying a wafer can remove that copy; the shipboard duplicate remains. Intentionally applying another person's imprint is not an established way to impersonate or control them. |

Yraina demonstrates the kit's verification and explains the steps. A brief voluntary test is available if wanted. Do not require a long experiment, an unwilling test subject, or another mission to validate a complete kit.

**Default preparation:** one kit and the working imprints travel with Yraina; a second kit and verified duplicates remain aboard under Nyrix's care. The third is spare if recovered. Stage Axiom, armor, weapons, communications, and any selected supplies together for Kaelith's retrieval. Physically pack salt now if they want it; its earlier mention did not put it in their pockets.

Set the gear retrieval against Kaelith's established table capabilities. This guide supplies no new spell power or arbitrary obstruction: the holding room is unwarded against Void, and the ship can remain at a suitable local berth. Test reach and staging before the wipe if range is uncertain. Do not assume an item-sized retrieval rift also transports the whole party.

Nyrix offers a practical support arrangement: Yraina reports after restoration, with an expected check-in within thirty minutes of entering. A missed report prompts contact and preparation to assist; a distress call triggers the agreed response. The players may revise that arrangement. The logged one-week promise remains the distant fallback, not a reason to ignore immediate trouble.

**Transition:** record where the ship is, who has each imprint, and what help is actually available. Yraina wipes Asha and Kaelith as agreed, retaining her own memories. Compress travel. The wipe changes available character knowledge; it does not give the GM control of their actions.

## Scene 2 — Household intake

**Scene goal:** Yraina's selected cover obtains internal holding, with a real administrative cost and identifiable witnesses.

**Present:** the trio, Neris, the intake guards, and a routine screening technician.

**Setting:** a reception counter, sample displays, seating, an equipment inspection tray, and two clearly marked doors: commercial appointments and household review. The servant declaration identifies Yraina as the sponsor responsible for the pair. Her kit is listed as restoration equipment. The declared wafers are checked for safe handling, not opened to search their contents.

Neris verifies Yraina against household records. Her family identity is genuine; it supplies a referral, **not the failed discretionary clearance**. Neris cannot approve a returning family member's personal-servant claim herself and sends it to Delven. The trio waits together because Yraina is responsible for their custody during review.

The routine magical examination checks for obvious bindings, as Yraina described. It does not read thoughts, identify the hidden plot, or decode everyone's history. The PCs really are wiped. Do not ask the players to pass Performance checks to make a genuine condition look genuine, and do not dictate how they experience it.

### Neris's questions and limits

She needs the sponsor, intended household placement, restoration equipment declaration, and a reason for the unresolved custody paperwork. Yraina supplies her real name and treats the review as a family matter. The PCs can speak, remain silent, object, or act according to their present understanding. Neris can ask for clarification without making an unconventional reaction proof of infiltration.

**Default admission:** the group receives one sponsor band and two dependent bands. These admit them to holding and household review; they do not open the research or executive doors. Neris schedules review and retains a record of Yraina's arrival. The kit remains with its declared operator.

### Adjustment for the Educator's surviving evidence

- **No attributable report:** ordinary referral, with the normal holding procedure below.
- **A suspicious but incomplete report:** Neris sends the description to Oren. Two guards move from the loading court to the holding corridor to check the group before release. They do not appear from a new reserve force.
- **A confirmed violent incident naming the trio:** Delven orders them held for his own interview. He wants Yraina alive and wants an account before reporting a family scandal to Kyrsa. Oren puts the same two guards in the corridor and locks the outer release. Yraina's status still wins a household room, not freedom. Their kit remains a listed treatment device; neither Delven nor Oren knows the extraction plan or that its wafers contain a mission briefing.

These are different degrees of containment, not guaranteed innocence. If the group openly attacks intake, resolve that changed situation using the forces already present. Do not restart the entry or force them to repeat it.

**Leave the scene when:** the group is in holding, or its actions have created an immediate confrontation. The useful established facts are internal access, the kit's whereabouts, who knows they are here, and the next review.

## Scene 3 — Holding and restoration

**Scene goal:** allow the planned restoration to work and put the next action back in the PCs' hands.

**Setting:** a household consultation room with three chairs, a wash station, a door status panel, and an internal review call button. It opens onto the household corridor. A labeled service door farther along that corridor leads toward allocation and freight handling.

The room has an occupancy sensor and door log, not continuous audio or a live memory feed. That privacy serves family sponsors who discuss valuable personal assets. Restoration equipment is permitted here; activating it does not itself alarm the building.

Neris gives an initial review estimate of fifteen minutes and a two-minute warning before an interviewer or escort comes to the door. An active pursuit the players brought with them can shorten that time, but show the incoming interruption. The standard window easily permits both restorations. Do not fabricate a malfunction to consume it.

Yraina explains what she is doing and offers the frame. If a wiped PC refuses, the player retains that choice: Yraina can explain, offer the preparation recording made aboard if they chose to make one, or wait. The operation's method does not authorize the GM to narrate compliance or force restoration.

Once memory access returns, Kaelith can attempt the staged gear retrieval using his actual ability. Axiom and the armor resume their relationships without filling in events they did not witness. Notify shipboard support if the group chooses to use the agreed check-in.

### Pressure after recovery

Neris will bring an escort for household review. The PCs can use that appointment, delay it, get the escort to take them toward records, interfere with the door, or leave under their own power. Their chosen servant entry has succeeded; they now decide how to exploit it.

Full combat gear will not pass as ordinary servant clothing if seen. The corridor is not constantly watched by a person, but its cameras can record an armed departure. Stealth, a credible escort instruction, controlled movement, or a deliberate reveal changes who reacts. Do not demand one particular solution.

**If the room is locked:** Neris or Delven can release it, the local controller can be interfered with, or the door can be physically defeated. A failed attempt reveals activity or costs time according to the method; it does not remove the other options. Guards remain where Oren assigned them.

**Useful discovery:** the appointment display names Delven and shows household review, specialist allocation, and Reserve Compatibility as internal destinations. This makes the next choices visible even if the group never cooperates with its escort.

**Leave the scene when:** they act beyond the room. A safe restoration and regained gear are an earned success, not the signal for an unavoidable ambush.

## Scene 4 — Specialist allocation

**Scene goal:** show what the retained expertise is building, introduce an unmistakable Sol reference, and provide practical access to the research and executive records.

**Present:** Teral, two ordinary administrative workers, and whichever guards have actually moved here. Neris may accompany a legitimate household referral.

**Setting:** an internal work office: placement boards, return appointments, locked imprint drawers, and a glazed service passage toward the laboratory. A freight schedule also identifies the loading court. Nothing requires the GM to describe suffering before the characters can learn useful information.

Teral initially wants the visitors processed and out of his queue. He has seen complaints routed back to the people who caused them. He will not assume armed strangers are liberators, but a request for specific records is something he can understand.

### What is available here

1. **Current assignments:** several specialists are repeatedly restored to produce and test neural-lattice sections for Reserve Compatibility, under Vesk's authority rather than Delven's ordinary budget. The scale exceeds ordinary implant orders, and the assembly manifests list no living recipient. The laboratory is active now. Teral knows the sections are being manufactured for a single larger system, but not what that system is intended to become.
2. **A concrete contradiction:** Delven suspended overtime and transfers to that department; an instruction bearing Kyrsa's approval reinstated them and barred ordinary cost review. This points to protected authority, not proof of mind control.
3. **Access:** Teral can issue a technical escort instruction and open the service-side laboratory door. Without his cooperation, the door can be accessed through staff credentials, its controller, or force. The visible directory still gives the destination.
4. **A next lead:** the work queue lists the transfer of the current comparison record to Vesk and an outbound courier booking for **Survey Exchange Nine**. The full booking is in executive correspondence, but the place name is not locked behind Teral's survival.
5. **An obvious old name in current work:** a live research request is headed **SOL / TERRA — HUMAN ORIGIN POWER STUDY**. It asks staff to send a lattice-capacity model to the protected research liaison. Teral can show who requested it and when, but cannot explain the power source. It is recent Maena business, not an ancient archive entry accidentally copied into the office.

### Teral's terms

He wants his personal work imprint and a written cancellation of his assignment. His case is in the local drawer; Neris or Delven can release it through ordinary authority. He can also identify it for the PCs if they decide to take it. His memory record does not contain the research's complete calibration and cannot substitute for the laboratory target.

He will explain ordinary schedules without a bargain. He risks his job to supply active access if the crew demonstrates that he can retain his imprint and leave. Threats may obtain compliance, but do not turn it into gratitude or allegiance. If he leaves, one coworker wants to stay and another wants copies of their own records; there is no unanimous staff uprising.

**Competing pressure:** a broad request for staff files brings the administrator's attention; opening a single known case may stay local. Oren responds to actual reports, not a metaphysical measure of how much the party has learned.

**Leave the scene when:** the crew has a destination or chooses what to do with the office. They may go directly to the laboratory, seek Delven, or leave with the available evidence. Do not force a rescue bargain as the price of continuing.

## Scene 5 — The neural-lattice laboratory

**Scene goal:** answer the party's central question: Maena intends to bring manufactured neural-lattice hardware to life with Spark and create the first god that did not begin as a mortal mage. Then offer ways to take evidence, stop the local research, or both.

**Present:** Meret, three technical staff, and the two guards assigned to research unless already moved or defeated. Vesk begins the operation in the director's suite; he comes here to collect the latest record if warned of an intrusion or asked to attend.

**Setting:** a cooled test chamber with shielded Spark sample cups, racks of precisely manufactured neural-lattice sections, an instrument bench, a removable recording cartridge, and a waist-high comparison assembly connected to the lattice test housing. A fabrication display shows how the sections fit into a much larger artificial neural structure. The side door reaches the service passage. Sample interlocks, shutdown controls, and a wheeled equipment cradle are visible before a fight.

The local sections are inert test hardware, not a conscious infant god. There is no completed being waiting to awaken when someone fires a weapon. The larger assembly's location is not established by seeing its design here.

Meret's first priority in a disturbance is stopping an unsafe test and getting people away from the bench. She demands to know whose authority the party carries but will explain an observable danger rather than let a technician die to preserve protocol.

### What the work reveals

The active design and readable work instructions establish the project in plain terms:

- **Manufactured neural substrate:** assemble the lattice as the basis of a new being. There is no mortal candidate to implant, restore, or elevate.
- **Spark-mediated animation:** use Spark to bring that hardware to life. This is the purpose of the reserve-compatibility trials.
- **Divine outcome:** the sponsored objective is an autonomous magitech god. The project explicitly contrasts that aim with the history of gods arising from living mortal mages. Its designers know they are attempting something unprecedented.

Meret can explain all three. She regards creating a new form of life as a legitimate scientific ambition; she does not know that the attempted birth threatens Ternus and future possibility. She can be proud, defensive, persuaded, or frightened according to what the PCs do. Do not make her innocently unaware of the goal she is directly researching.

Tests show that samples from the accumulated reserve respond to ordinary industrial uses but fail the intended lattice-animation process. The team suspects imposed interference it cannot remove. **Its records do not identify the Hand by name.** Asha and Kaelith can connect this to Kek's account themselves. Vesk's instructions require a workaround without replacing the reserve.

An engineering memorandum also admits that independence, stability, and loyalty after successful animation are unproven. Maena wants its own god; this does not establish that it can program divine obedience. Axiom's emerging personhood is a relevant comparison if the players make it, not proof that ordinary Eldimagus creation already accomplishes the same thing.

Evidence of the anticipated wider harm can make Meret stop cooperating with Vesk. She will explain a safe shutdown even while refusing to abandon her life's work. The PCs can also secure the diagrams and protocols without winning her trust. **Simply understanding and preserving these findings fulfills the central discovery objective.**

### The laboratory's Sol clue

One visible reference pane is headed **SOL / TERRA — ORIGINAL HUMAN HOMEWORLD**. Its current planning note states that Maena's origin research identifies a source of extraordinary power there and requests estimates of how much a manufactured neural substrate could sustain. This is a commissioned research question, not proof that Maena can reach or use the source.

Meret knows that Vesk supplied the reference and that the executive office keeps its source assessment. She does not know the source's true nature. The pane and a copy in the result cartridge make the connection easy to preserve even if the party never visits Delven. Neither contains the phrase “most powerful Catalyst,” a route to Terra, or a technical explanation of the sealed system.

### The target and the actual payoff

| Asset | Function | What taking or destroying it achieves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Calibrated comparison assembly** | Maintains the particular reference conditions needed to compare successive experiments. Its difficult part is the accumulated physical calibration, not a replaceable power supply. | Stops the current experimental series. Rebuilding and recalibrating requires a replacement specialist team and at least two weeks of uninterrupted work. |
| **Current result cartridge** | Holds fine-grained measurements, adjustment history, the lattice-animation protocol, the sponsored divine objective, and the SOL/TERRA reference pane. | Supplies strong evidence of Maena's purpose and its interest in humanity's origin; taking the only current full record also denies results to the operator. Maena cannot reconstruct these from the daily summaries alone. |
| **Latest analysis, understood by the staff** | Meret and her technicians can interpret the rig and results. | Willing cooperation preserves understanding for the crew; removing equipment alone still damages Maena. No one has to be forced aboard as loot. |

**Known remote copies:** Vesk has already sent earlier summary reports. They preserve broad progress, not the current calibration or complete measurements. This is fixed before the raid. There is no surprise intact duplicate elsewhere after the PCs succeed.

**Working timings:** ordinary safe shutdown takes one minute; removing the secured cartridge then takes another minute; detaching the calibrated assembly takes five minutes with Meret's cooperation or suitable technical work. The assembly fits its wheeled cradle and a cargo lift. Copying the current results takes two minutes at the powered console. A powered-off cartridge can simply be carried away for later reading.

These are new encounter timings, not a newly discovered countdown to catastrophe. Shooting the exposed assembly is faster and irreversibly ruins its calibration; breaking the outer housing alone merely damages a repairable enclosure. Make the difference legible through Meret, labels, or examination before the players commit.

Contained Spark samples are small quantities isolated from the moon's reserve. Rupturing them can create a local hazardous discharge; use the table's normal hazard rules. Shutdown closes their shutters. There is no civilization-ending laboratory explosion hidden behind an ordinary attack roll.

### NPC response and failure

Meret trades cooperation for safe handling and room for her staff to withdraw. Research guards protect access, not individual test tubes. If Oren orders evacuation, they help remove personnel; Vesk instead prioritizes the cartridge. Those priorities can collide without everyone becoming an ally.

A failed technical attempt costs time or exposes the work to whoever is responding. It does not retroactively make the data vanish. If Vesk gets the cartridge, the assembly remains a meaningful target and his escape is tracked physically through Scene 7.

**Leave the scene when:** the party decides what it has learned and what to do with the opportunity, or abandons the attempt. Carry the exact state of the rig, cartridge, copies, and staff forward. Obtaining the god-making evidence is a success in its own right; sabotage adds a material setback for Maena.

## Scene 6 — The director and the protected liaison

**Scene goal:** establish why Maena sponsors the manufactured-god project, reveal its knowledge of power in Terra/Sol, and make its takeover plan and offsite command relationship actionable.

**Present:** Delven, Vesk unless he has moved, and Oren if his duties allow. Their guards come from the shared roster. If the party arrived through household review, this can occur before Scenes 4 or 5.

**Setting:** an executive workroom and a small correspondence office. A live operations display shows the loading court and laboratory status; a secure desk terminal holds the operating agreement. A side exit leads to the same service passage as the laboratory. This is a place to negotiate, seize records, or fight, not a required throne-room encounter.

### Delven's position

Delven recognizes Yraina as a serious family problem. He wants to learn what she is doing and contain it before Kyrsa hears a damaging version. Her relationship earns attention, not permission to seize the building.

If the crew has demonstrated control of people, evidence, or research, he can bargain. His first useful offer is **safe passage and a narrow equipment loss recorded as an internal dispute**. In return he wants public silence and the appearance that he remains in charge. The PCs can refuse, demand more, deceive him, or attack. He is not immune because he is the negotiator.

His most exploitable grievance is specific: Vesk can commandeer specialists and reverse spending decisions, but Delven signs for the losses. Show the actual conflicting orders. Delven knows the chain is abnormal; he does not know Kyrsa is magically bound.

### The executive answer: why Maena is doing this

The current sponsorship file is headed **CONSTRUCTED DIVINITY — NEURAL LATTICE ANIMATION PROGRAM**. It is readable with executive access or after the desk is secured. It states the same core aim as the laboratory: use manufactured hardware and Spark to produce a living god without a mortal precursor.

The commercial explanation is equally direct. In this operation's preparation, Maena expects influence from controlling the manufacture, first resources, and institutional upbringing of such a being. The sponsors treat divine independence as a management problem they expect to solve. The technical annex admits they have not demonstrated obedience, stability, or even successful animation. Their confidence and their evidence do not match.

Delven knows the promised outcome. He has rationalized his part as securing supplies and political protection for a research achievement. Vesk treats successful animation as a program requirement, but cannot truthfully guarantee that the new god would serve Maena. This gives the PCs several positions to challenge without requiring an NPC villain speech.

The file and laboratory provide independent evidence of the objective. If the crew arrives here first, this is the central reveal; the physical laboratory then offers verification and a target for intervention.

### Obvious evidence of knowledge about Terra and Sol

A separately tabbed annex is titled **TERRA / SOL — HUMAN ORIGIN: POWER SOURCE ASSESSMENT**. It contains a current assessment, signed expenditure for continuing the inquiry, and instructions to circulate relevant findings through Vesk's protected channel.

Its plain conclusions are:

- Terra is the original human homeworld, associated with Sol; the origin references are being treated as real places, not metaphor or discredited myth.
- Maena knows extraordinary power remains associated with that origin. Its assessment treats the existence of that power as established and worth pursuing despite the apparent loss of access; it does not share the full basis of that knowledge with this office.
- The source may be relevant to the limits of the manufactured-god project, but access, compatibility, and exploitation remain open research questions.

This is **evidence of Maena's informed interest**, not proof of every claim in its assessment. Do not place a functioning route, a verified present-day map, or the Catalyst's identity and cosmic ranking in the annex. Maena's precise original source of knowledge remains beyond these local records. The copy includes the Survey Exchange Nine distribution address, giving the PCs a practical way to pursue who knows more.

The reference should be hard to overlook once the office is searched. An ordinary examination of the project files reveals both headings. If Delven is bargaining, knowledge of the homeworld can be something he offers; it is not available only if he survives or confesses.

### The prepared takeover

The desk file is titled **Emergency Reserve Stewardship Agreement**. It contains three practical elements:

- A false certification that Saezo's management cannot safeguard the reserve. Attached source logs contradict the incident account; Delven's office assembled the case.
- A list of three outside countersignatories and the order in which they must approve it. One has signed. The other two have not. The existing signature's cover note requires the signed packet to pass through this office's registered execution channel.
- Named replacement managers, arrival instructions, and pump-house access requests to be presented after ratification. The proposed transfer still requires operators and physical access; this document does not remotely own every valve.

**Ways this raid can obstruct it:** seize the original execution seal and packet; make Delven register a withdrawal; disable the dedicated execution channel; or transmit the contradiction evidence to the pending countersignatories and the actual pump-house management. The seal is a local corporate execution device, not the sealed soul repository. No automatic connection to Yraina's failed invocation exists.

Taking the seal and packet prevents this office from completing the current prepared ratification. The outside signatory retains its own copy, so capture is a delay and loss of a ready plan, not erasure from history. Replacing the execution authority requires a new appointment and resubmission. A registered withdrawal or convincing authenticated disclosure can collapse the current case more thoroughly.

Merely copying the file is intelligence, not sabotage. If the PCs want disruption, make those distinctions clear. If they simply destroy the desk, local files and devices suffer according to their actual location; do not pretend a fire selectively removes every external copy.

### Vesk and the hidden authority

Vesk initially uses protected status and Kyrsa's name. When challenged, he demands that Delven secure the research rather than discuss its purpose. He tries to keep the reporting packet out of the crew's hands.

His operational packet contains:

1. **A routing record:** lattice-animation results and the SOL/TERRA assessment go through **Survey Exchange Nine**, an offsite relay and transfer station reached through a filed Umbral transit approach. The record includes navigational instructions, a receiving contact, and the next scheduled delivery. This is a route to the relay, not to Sol or Terra.
2. **A protected instruction:** the recipient, identified in correspondence as **the Custodian**, directs Vesk to keep the artificial-divinity program and its reserve-workaround effort independent of the local director and report resistance by household leadership. “Custodian” is an operational title, not a revealed ancient name. Sharing a reporting channel does not establish that the Custodian originated all knowledge of Terra or personally controls its power.
3. **A command contradiction:** Kyrsa had endorsed a suspension of the current line of work. After a private consultation arranged through the same channel, her office reversed it and placed Vesk beyond local review. This establishes influence and a useful inquiry, not proof of the exact binding.

**GM truth for this operation:** the Custodian is the ancient Necrotis follower. Exchange Nine is its relay, not a guaranteed location of its body, Kyrsa, or the mines. The receiving contact is **Dock Warden Senn**, an administrator who handles sealed research transfers. No powers or secret lineage are assigned to Senn here.

Vesk knows the Custodian outranks ordinary management in practice and takes part in its coercive work. He does not know how Kyrsa is bound. Capturing him gives an informed route into the organization, not the entire answer. His packet is also available through Delven's correspondence cache, so a refusal to talk or his death does not erase the lead. Teral's schedule supplied the relay's name; the laboratory's shipping console holds the same booked destination and approach information.

### If the scene becomes a fight

Delven seeks cover and an exit or a bargain. Oren tries to separate intruders from the principals. Vesk tries to get the packet and, if available, the current cartridge out through the service passage. Resolve those attempts in ordinary play. He does not disappear between scene transitions.

Vesk is not the operation's boss caster; use him as a trained agent with a sidearm and local credentials. The warder and spirits described below are separate security assets, not newly summoned reinforcements attached to his survival.

**Leave the scene when:** the party has secured answers or a lead, acted on the agreement, made a bargain, or forced a change in local leadership. No confession, sabotage, or duel is required before extraction.

## Scene 7 — Extraction and consequences

**Scene goal:** resolve who and what actually gets out, and let a victory remain a victory.

The party has two established physical exits: back through intake, or through the service passage to the loading court. The court contains an enclosed cargo lift and a utility carrier suitable for the laboratory cradle and passengers. Keys and dispatch control are held at the loading desk; taking transport can be negotiated, coerced, bypassed, or done by force.

Nyrix and available crew can meet a communicated extraction plan. Do not invent a transport range, mass limit, or new Void power to force a chase or waive one. If Kaelith's established magic can extract the group and chosen cargo, it works. If it only retrieves staged objects, it does not silently become a mass-evacuation gate. The raid should survive either ruling because physical exits exist.

### What pursues them

Only personnel still able and ordered to act can pursue. Oren prioritizes recovering irreplaceable equipment and preventing further deaths; if the site is lost, he can stop spending people on it. Delven can order local disengagement as part of a bargain. Vesk might flee with stolen results, but has to reach and leave the same loading court as everyone else.

If outside response was called, show the acknowledged request, expected arrival, or actual inbound vehicle. Do not generate another squad every time the PCs win. An intact laboratory, a successful negotiation, severed communications, and a destroyed command post produce different exits.

### End-state record

- **Primary discoveries:** does the crew know that Maena manufactures neural-lattice hardware, intends Spark to bring it to life, and seeks the first god without a mortal origin? Record what evidence or testimony supports each conclusion and what copies they retain.
- **Terra/Sol:** which explicit references did they see or take? They can establish Maena's knowledge of extraordinary power at humanity's origin. The most-powerful-Catalyst truth remains GM-only unless separately revealed in play; the closed route and Sol's apparent ending remain unresolved.
- **Research:** assembly intact, removed, disabled, or destroyed; cartridge held by whom; copies made; willing specialists aboard or elsewhere. Losing the physical calibration creates the stated two-week minimum rebuild, longer without staff. Seizing only results denies current measurements but leaves Meret able to collect more if the rig survives.
- **Takeover:** merely discovered, execution interrupted, formally withdrawn, or publicly challenged. Record which parties received proof. Do not mark the whole succession threat solved by a copied document.
- **Exposure:** who can identify Asha, Kaelith, and Yraina; what was actually sent to Kyrsa or the Custodian; which communications the party stopped.
- **People:** who chose to leave, who remains in custody, who died, and who now holds each personal imprint. Freeing someone does not compel them to join the crew.
- **Next lead:** Survey Exchange Nine and its usable approach record. If no one took the packet, a surviving schedule, staff account, or laboratory booking still permits further investigation; complete destruction can genuinely lose that evidence.

The larger program survives because it exists beyond this site. Do not restore the removed rig, negate a registered withdrawal, or invent a perfect secret backup to preserve the original threat unchanged. Kyrsa can react later through the information and institutions that remain.

## Shared security roster and escalation

Use one roster across all scenes. These are encounter roles and counts, not new combat statistics. Use the table's normal competent security, veteran, and Spirit-mage profiles; the local force should threaten a careless party while remaining beatable by this well-equipped crew.

| Asset | Starting position | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Two intake guards** | Visitor entrance | Hold the entrance and respond to the registrar. They do not automatically leave the public area for every internal alarm. |
| **Two research guards** | Laboratory access | Control the restricted door and assist staff evacuation or protection of the rig. |

The sentries are a security force, not evidence that every employee or worker is bound. The ward vessel permits controlled recall and containment. If it is destroyed while they are deployed, they are not automatically destroyed too: their behavior follows the table's existing Spirit rules. Explain that risk through the warder's handling or examination before the crew chooses to smash it. Salt obstructs passage temporarily; humans can disturb the line, and it does not bind or command a spirit.

**Escalation follows reports and visible acts:**

1. **Routine:** appointment and custody controls. No enemies attack because the GM has reached Scene 4.
2. **Inquiry:** an inconsistent instruction or attributable report reaches Oren. He checks the relevant door, asks for identities, and assigns the loading pair if needed. This is an opportunity for explanation, diversion, evasion, or preemption.
3. **Containment:** confirmed armed intrusion or violence. Oren closes the affected function, moves the warder, and coordinates existing guards. Life-safety exits can be opened locally from inside; locking a security door does not seal everyone into a death trap.
4. **Loss response:** substantial theft or loss of command. Vesk attempts to secure the research record, Delven seeks a controllable settlement, and Oren may request outside security. An acknowledged request brings **one six-person relief squad no sooner than fifteen in-world minutes later**, arriving at the loading court. If the request never gets out, the squad does not arrive. This is the only external reinforcement in the operation.

Transitions are not one per scene or one per failed roll. Track how an observer could notice and communicate. Communications loss interrupts new reports; it does not erase those already sent. Existing cameras can support later identification even if no one watched them live.

## Adjudication notes

Use [[Rules/02-basic-mechanics-and-concepts|Basic Mechanics]] for the local DT scale. Routine operation with the right access needs no roll. For improvised approaches, **DT 2** suits a moderately protected controller or hurried copying under pressure; **DT 3** suits a contested authorization, technical removal while threatened, or convincing a hostile official to accept real personal risk. Use the actual character skill and method. Combat follows ordinary defenses and initiative, not these investigative thresholds.

Give basic visible information and readable records without rolls. Checks buy speed, discretion, deeper interpretation, or access the character does not already possess. Failed attempts create a specific consequence—time, a recorded irregularity, an alerted person, damaged equipment—rather than withholding every route to a necessary fact.

The compulsory setup is limited to the GM-approved servant entry. Once the party is inside, its route, dialogue, treatment of NPCs, appetite for combat, and decision to leave remain the players' choices. These are GM notes, not read-aloud narration.
