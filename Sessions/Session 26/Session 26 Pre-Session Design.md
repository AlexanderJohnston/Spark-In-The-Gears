# Session 26 Scratchpad

## Design stance

This is a high-level session framework, not a sequence of scenes.

- Preserve the exact Session 25 endpoint and let the players decide how long they remain on the Flame, whom they brief, and where they go next.
- Prepare pressures, interests, costs, and useful discoveries rather than a correct route.
- Do not write a fixed infiltration, negotiation, combat, or travel sequence until the players choose an operational direction.
- Do not use the Web to answer the strategic decisions the players have just earned the right to make.
- Treat Session 25's revelations as meaningful progress. Complicate their application without immediately declaring all of Kek's information false.

## Session thesis

**The enemy is clear; the problem is keeping two unacceptable actors from acting first.**

Session 25 gave Asha and Kaelith the moral simplicity they wanted. Maena is exploiting the dead, stripping divine remains, widening a wound toward the age of the Titans, and deliberately pursuing the birth that ends possibility. The Hand opposes that outcome. Saezo is an ignorant pawn.

The tactical truth is less comfortable: Saezo's belief that he remains in control is currently part of what keeps Ternus alive. If that belief breaks, he will dump the moon's entire Spark reserve into the Catalyst core buried in its engine and attempt to create a god. Maena cannot currently use the reserve because the Hand's weapon tainted it, but they are working toward a solution. If given time, they will politically assassinate Saezo, seize his Spark infrastructure, and resume their own birth plan.

Neither party can be allowed to proceed. The crew needs to preserve the present deadlock long enough to dismantle the engine, reserve, hidden program, or dependencies that make either move catastrophic.

The desired movement is:

**revelation -> ownership -> doctrine -> commitment -> first consequence**

The session is successful if the players leave it with a strategy they chose, a clear understanding of what that strategy protects and risks, and at least one relationship changed by whom they trusted with the truth.

## Exact opening state

### Physical state

- Asha, Kaelith, and Nara are on the bridge of the **Flame of Knowledge**.
- Kek is at the temporal matrix, containing the Web's madness as Guardian.
- The Swarm surrounds and controls the route to and from the bridge. It granted an audience; it did not become friendly or obedient.
- The bridge hangs over the Flame's white fire Catalyst. Neither PC connected directly to the Web.
- The rest of the crew remains aboard the refitted **Lone Star** in the Xal system.
- Nara brought the group to the Flame and is the most likely means of immediate extraction, but she does not command the vessel or the Swarm.
- Two ancient emergency corvettes remain in the docking bay as an uncertain fallback.

### Character state

- **Asha** has moved from shock into cheerful moral certainty: Maena's operation is an enemy she does not feel obliged to morally accommodate. She favors the Trade Moon partly because it sounds more engaging than Ternus.
- **Kaelith** believes they finally have a direction. The mining of spirits and divine remains directly violates his understanding of the Great Cycle and has intensified his desire to learn Spirit magic.
- **Nara** immediately proposed using the Web to locate and erase Maena leadership. She favors decisive, intelligence-led violence and may underestimate how distributed the operation is.
- **Kek** remains lucid enough to advise them, but is exhausted, Web-exposed, and responsible for containment. He is not a continuing mission-control oracle.
- **Axiom** has completed its first catalyzation through Xal. It is still pre-verbal, but can perceive absence, hidden space, boundaries, and displacement through its active Void baseline.

### Resources and unfinished commitments

- The Lone Star's extensive refit is complete. Its single catalyzation remains unchosen.
- Axiom's next prescribed catalyzation is **Aashitak / Dream**. There is no established deadline.
- The old Terra/Hand disk remains in Aran's custody for protected storage associated with the Flame. The log does not establish that it has been catalogued in the Archive.
- Rourke still withholds the requested freighter file and wants the disk returned or at least wants to be consulted before anything is made public.
- Yraina believes she can get a message before the Maena Matriarch, potentially at personal cost.
- Saezo paid the crew's obligations, equipped them, refitted the Lone Star, and expressed interest in future work against Maena. The crew has direct access to him that does not yet look hostile.

## GM-only campaign rulings after Session 25

These are authoritative campaign truths. They are not automatically player knowledge.

### Saezo's failure state

- Saezo has accumulated an immense reservoir of liquid Spark inside the Trade Moon's pumping infrastructure.
- Deep within the moon is an engine built around a Catalyst core.
- If Saezo believes he has lost control, he will pump the entire Spark reserve into that core in an attempt to create a new god.
- He does not understand the larger Maena design or the true nature of the birth. He believes this is his project and his means of securing permanent control.
- The Hand-tainted Spark does not make this action safe. Kek's averaged futures show that Saezo proceeding can still cost the Ternus homeworld.

### What control means to Saezo

Saezo is reassured by evidence that systems, people, and rivals remain responsive to his leverage.

- Product continues moving through infrastructure he controls.
- His money and access can still overrule restrictions.
- The crew remains reachable and appears to treat him as a relevant patron rather than a disposable enemy.
- Maena appears containable, divided, or vulnerable to action he helped enable.
- Problems arrive as information he can act upon rather than unexplained losses imposed upon him.

Kaelith's staff invoice is the clearest prior example. Maena Biologic Interface Group used the trade network to deny release of a **Spark-compatible neural lattice** and the multi-Catalyst materials Orrel required. Yraina took the order under the table; Saezo later paid the 285-million-credit invoice without hesitation. To him, converting Maena's “no” into his own completed transaction was proof of control.

Likely threats to that belief include:

- discovering that his Spark is unusable or outside his command;
- suffering a public political defeat he cannot answer;
- losing the pumping network, engine, or reserve without understanding how;
- learning that trusted assets have been acting against him;
- seeing Maena close around him while every apparent ally withdraws.

### The blocked Maena plan

- The Hand's freighter weapon tainted **all Spark currently stored on the Trade Moon**.
- This is why Maena cannot proceed with its original birth plan now.
- The corruption is a delaying weapon, not a permanent victory. Maena will eventually understand or circumvent it.
- Maena's intended solution includes politically assassinating Saezo, seizing legal and operational control of his Spark, and using the captured infrastructure for their own purpose.
- Saezo and Maena therefore threaten the same reserve from opposite directions: his loss of control triggers an impulsive birth attempt; their eventual takeover enables a deliberate one.

### The Matriarch and the hidden operator

- The Maena Matriarch is the principal beneficiary and public center of the hidden program, but she is not its free architect.
- The true operator is an ancient surviving follower of Necrotis.
- That follower has placed a unique Spirit binding on the Matriarch by feeding her a combination of **Nefarius blood and Necrotis blood**.
- The mixed divine source prevents the binding from presenting like an ordinary Spirit compulsion under the usual detection methods.
- Nefarius blood alone is established as Void Catalyst material and does not normally exert meaningful control over those who use it. The coercion is bespoke work created by the follower's combination and binding, not generic “Nefarius corruption.”
- The Matriarch's authority, appetites, and institutional self-interest remain real. The binding directs and exploits them rather than reducing her to a visibly mindless puppet.
- Removing the Matriarch without understanding the binding may free the operator, transfer control, activate a contingency, or leave the program intact behind a new beneficiary.

Official setting material supports the operator's underlying tradition: Necrotis was an unliving goddess who ruled the raised *Maker's Wrath*, used necromancers and soul manipulation, and pursued victory through infiltration, coercion, attrition, and the permanent removal of souls from the Great Cycle. Use that inheritance to shape the follower's methods. Do not make the follower a loud death cultist when patient capture of institutions is more faithful to both the lore and the campaign plan.

### Kek's truth model

- Kek is broadly correct, but he speaks from the average pattern across many futures.
- His account identifies recurring actors, pressures, and outcomes more reliably than the exact state of this one reality.
- A mechanism common to most branches may be incomplete, delayed, or altered here.
- Kek has also chosen not to be completely honest about everything he knows.
- If pressed about his certainty—or especially about Terra—he may admit that he is withholding information.
- He knows a little about Terra. What he knows is too terrifying for him to speak plainly.
- This admission should establish an honesty boundary, not become another large revelation during Session 26.

## What Session 25 established

The distinction between fact, strong intelligence, and interpretation matters because almost all of the strategic revelation came through one damaged but uniquely capable source.

### Directly experienced or firmly established in play

- The Web tracks branching possibilities rather than parallel completed histories.
- Possibility collapses at the birth on the Trade Moon; if that thread completes, other threads are consumed.
- In the current line, the freighter reached the dock and exploded. That was the unusual outcome.
- Axiom accepted Xal and displayed its first deliberate judgment.
- Saezo controls an industrial Spark operation on the Trade Moon and receives enormous quantities from Ternus.
- Maena methods were present in the ancient Spirit binding on the freighter, although the identity of the responsible individual remains unknown.

### High-value intelligence supplied by Kek

- In most doomed branches, Saezo causes the relevant explosion; in this one, the crew's interventions kept him from panicking long enough for another weapon to reach its target.
- The Hand of Eterra planted ancient magic on the freighter to infect or corrupt Spark before it reached the Trade Moon.
- Maena arranged the freighter; the Hand interfered with it and likely helped position Asha and Kaelith to deliver the weapon.
- Saezo is attempting to create a Catalyst, not knowingly birth a god, and is a clueless pawn in the larger plan.
- Maena helps Ternus produce Spark and does not need to buy it.
- Maena wants the god to rise and is pursuing a greater source of power beyond the Web.
- Deep in the Umbral, Maena mines dead souls and the remains of gods to widen a hole toward the beginning and the time of the Titans.
- Destroying Maena or stopping Spark can prevent the birth, but triggering Saezo's plan can still cost the Ternus homeworld even with the Hand's corruption in place.

### Interpretations that remain open

- What Saezo believes his Catalyst will accomplish.
- Which observable threshold causes Saezo to conclude that control is lost and begin the dump.
- How quickly the full reserve can be moved into the moon's Catalyst core once he commits.
- Whether the “heart” of the Trade Moon operation is the reserve, pumping network, engine, Catalyst core, control authority, or the relationship between them.
- How much of the Maena family and its companies knows about the hidden program.
- How close the Necrotis follower is to cracking the Hand's corruption.
- What the mixed Nefarius/Necrotis binding looks like to different forms of magical perception and what can expose it safely.
- How much the Hand knew about Asha and Kaelith and which of their choices it actually influenced.
- Whether all Ternan Spark comes from the same Umbral process or whether multiple stages and feedstocks have been conflated.
- The identity and nature of the thing or state Maena ultimately hopes to reach beyond the Web.
- What Kek knows about Terra and why even acknowledging it frightens him.

### Authority ruling for play

**Kek is broadly correct about the actors and causal structure.** His account is an average of many realities rather than a perfect observation of this one. Put uncertainty in whether a recurring mechanism applies exactly here, not in whether Session 25 mattered. If challenged, Kek can distinguish “this usually happens” from “I have directly seen that it is happening now.”

## What the session should accomplish

Prioritize three functions.

1. **Return strategic agency to the players.** Nara and the Hand both manipulated them “for the greater good.” The crew now chooses its own intervention.
2. **Convert knowledge into an operating doctrine.** Decide what must remain stable, what can be risked, and what means are unacceptable even against Maena.
3. **Select the first layer of the problem to attack.** The first objective should gain leverage or understanding, not require the players to solve Spark and destroy Maena in one session.

Secondary functions, only if they arise naturally:

- Give the rest of the crew a chance to matter after two major revelations occurred with only Asha and Kaelith present.
- Let Axiom's first catalyzation have a practical but bounded consequence.
- Revisit the Lone Star's catalyzation only if Asha treats it as part of readiness for the chosen strategy.
- Establish the Hand as a dangerous possible ally, not a surprise benefactor or a new quest-giver.

## The central strategic constraint

**Saezo must believe he remains in control while the crew prevents Maena from taking that control.**

This is not the same as protecting, trusting, obeying, or absolving him. It means preventing him from ordering the full Spark dump while also preventing Maena from politically removing him and inheriting the same reserve.

The constraint should create play rather than forbid action:

- Saezo's access can become cover for an investigation.
- His ignorance can be exploited, challenged, or gradually corrected.
- His belief that the crew may work against Maena gives them a legitimate reason to remain close.
- His patronage creates a moral cost: maintaining normal relations can look and feel like complicity.
- The earlier invoice fight gives the crew a proven language for reassuring him: let him believe he has overruled Maena and turned one of their denials into his own success.
- Killing, exposing, humiliating, cornering, or visibly dispossessing him can move him toward the dump or clear Maena's path to succession.

The players should be able to learn the practical boundaries of “keep him in control.” Do not make the dump an invisible fail condition. Show Saezo consolidating authority, testing loyalty, protecting pump access, or preparing the core before he reaches the final order.

### The intended strategic shape: controlled disarmament

The deadlock is temporary. The crew is not trying to preserve Saezo's rule or the Maena status quo forever.

Their deeper task is to separate four conditions that are currently bundled together:

1. Saezo's belief that he controls the Trade Moon's future.
2. Saezo's actual ability to dump the full reserve into the Catalyst core.
3. Maena's political ability to remove him and inherit the infrastructure.
4. The Hand's taint preventing that infrastructure from producing Maena's intended result.

The ideal intervention lets Saezo keep enough visible authority that he does not panic while quietly reducing his real ability to complete the dump; keeps Maena blocked while exposing the succession attempt; and preserves the taint until the crew has a safer replacement. The players do not have to adopt this ideal, but it gives the GM a coherent way to judge partial successes.

Do not begin Session 26 with Maena's assassination already in motion unless the players immediately expose or destabilize Saezo. Its early expression should be political capacity and positioning: the same kind of trade authority that denied Kaelith's invoice, now applied to isolate a rival. Escalate only when Maena gains reason to accelerate.

## Decisions in front of the players

These are questions, not a checklist they must complete.

### What is the first objective?

- Map the liquid Spark reserve, pumping network, deep engine, and Catalyst core well enough to separate or control one dependency.
- Map Ternus's Spark production before touching the Trade Moon network.
- Learn what Saezo's Catalyst plan actually requires and what would trigger it.
- Find or force communication with the Hand to understand its weapon and next move.
- Identify a fracture inside Maena that can be exploited.
- Prepare power, allies, or contingencies before approaching any of the above.

### Who receives the truth?

- Only Asha, Kaelith, Nara, and Kek currently possess the whole Session 25 account.
- The Lone Star crew cannot make an informed contribution unless Asha and Kaelith brief them.
- Aran and the wider Xal circle should not automatically inherit Nara's knowledge.
- Yraina is a possible route into Maena and a person with her own family history, not just a communications device.
- Rourke can mobilize Ternan resources but is controlling, compromised by secrecy, and still bargaining for the disk.
- Saezo can provide unique access but may be the most dangerous person to surprise.

### What must remain stable while they act?

- Saezo's confidence or at least his lack of panic.
- The Hand's corruption in the Spark supply, unless the crew understands how it works.
- The political deadlock preventing Maena from removing Saezo and taking his reserve.
- The concealment of the Web and the Flame.
- The crew's apparent relationship with the Trade Moon.
- Civilian access to whatever infrastructure currently depends on Spark.

The players may choose that some of these are expendable. Make the consequence visible, not the choice impossible.

### What distinguishes them from the people using them?

Nara manipulated them to preserve possibility. The Hand manipulated them to deliver a weapon. Maena treats lives and souls as material. The party is now considering deception, assassination, and systemic sabotage for a worthy reason.

The important question is not whether they remain pure. It is whether they establish consent, limits, accountability, or protection for the people who will bear the cost of their plan.

## Modular strategic directions

None is the mandatory route. The party may combine them, reverse them, or invent another approach.

### Direction A: Shadow the Trade Moon pipeline

**Purpose:** Use the crew's existing relationship with Saezo and legitimate presence on the Trade Moon to map the reserve-to-core system while preserving his sense of control and detecting Maena's move against him.

**What makes it attractive:**

- It follows Kek's most direct advice.
- Saezo already trusts the crew enough to fund and equip them.
- The crew has seen his Spark infrastructure and has an established reason to pursue Maena on his behalf.
- Kaelith's blocked invoice gives them a proven example of the kind of victory over Maena that reassures Saezo.
- Asha explicitly found this direction more appealing.

**Core tension:** They may need to protect Saezo's authority from Maena without strengthening his ability or confidence enough to carry out the Spark dump on his own terms.

**A useful gain from this direction:** The party identifies one separable dependency between the liquid reserve, pump network, deep engine, and Catalyst core—something that can be controlled without making Saezo visibly lose everything at once.

**Costs to keep available:** Complicity in maintaining Spark commerce, politically defending Saezo, greater obligations to him, exposure to Maena surveillance, or the need to give him an apparent victory that increases his reach.

### Direction B: Trace Spark upstream on Ternus

**Purpose:** Learn how Spark is produced, who authorizes it, and which production stage connects ordinary industry to Umbral extraction.

**What makes it attractive:**

- It attacks the supply rather than the Trade Moon symptom.
- Rourke wants leverage over the crew and controls a relevant archive.
- The freighter file and the disk remain unresolved bargaining pieces.
- Kaelith's interest in the Great Cycle and Spirit makes the source personally significant.

**Core tension:** Disrupting upstream supply may be exactly the unexplained failure that causes Saezo to panic.

**A useful gain from this direction:** The party separates the visible Ternan production chain from the hidden Umbral stage and learns who can interrupt one without automatically collapsing the other.

**Costs to keep available:** Ternan state attention, moral responsibility for Spark-dependent infrastructure, loss of access if Rourke feels cheated, or discovery that many workers are coerced and ignorant rather than willing participants.

### Direction C: Treat the Hand as a dangerous ally

**Purpose:** Understand the freighter weapon, preserve or reproduce its protective effect, and determine what the Hand expects the crew to do next.

**What makes it attractive:**

- The Hand has already demonstrated that it understands Maena's ancient system better than the current factions do.
- It shares the immediate goal of preventing the birth.
- The old disk, freighter residue, erased SOL record, or Axiom's boundary sense may provide ways to recognize its work.

**Core tension:** Contact rewards the same hidden actor that already used the crew without consent and may happily sacrifice them again.

**A useful gain from this direction:** The party learns what the weapon changed in the Spark and what must remain untouched for that protection to hold.

**Costs to keep available:** A demand with no explanation, another manipulated delivery, conflict with Ternus or Xal, or proof that the Hand's acceptable casualty count is far higher than the crew's.

### Direction D: Find a fault line inside Maena

**Purpose:** Use Yraina's route, corporate records, or family rivalries to separate the Matriarch's interests from the hidden operator directing them and expose the control relationship without triggering succession.

**What makes it attractive:**

- It may avoid treating an entire family and its dependents as a single target.
- The Matriarch possesses the authority and benefits around which the program is organized and may know the actual trade agreement.
- Internal opposition can offer access that an external assault cannot.

**Core tension:** The Matriarch is simultaneously beneficiary, compromised decision-maker, and political keystone. Warning, curing, exposing, or killing her can each advance the Necrotis follower's contingencies.

**A useful gain from this direction:** The party finds a repeatable contradiction between the Matriarch's authentic interests and the decisions imposed by the mixed-blood binding, providing a way to prove influence without yet breaking it.

**Costs to keep available:** Yraina's autonomy, exposure to the binding, a loyalty test, political obligation to protect the Matriarch, or the need to leave a compromised ruler in place while isolating her controller.

### Direction E: Immediate decapitation or hard containment

**Purpose:** Remove a leader, operator, facility, pump dependency, or political capability before either side can complete its move.

**What makes it attractive:**

- Nara can support targeted violence and may be able to locate people through means short of another direct Web consultation.
- Asha and Kaelith both ended Session 25 emotionally ready for decisive action.
- It denies the enemy time to adapt if the crew's information is already compromised.

**Core tension:** Killing Saezo clears Maena's succession path unless the reserve and engine are already secured. Killing the Matriarch may not remove the hidden operator or binding architecture. Striking infrastructure can convince Saezo he has lost control and trigger the full dump.

**How to honor this choice:** Do not simply veto it. Require the players to name what continuity, trigger, or civilian consequence they are accepting. If they proceed, let the strike succeed against the chosen target and make the surviving system react logically.

**A useful gain from this direction:** A target is genuinely removed and one part of the operation stops, while the response reveals whether it was a controller, replaceable administrator, political keystone, or restraint on the rival plan.

### Direction F: Prepare before choosing a theater

**Purpose:** Brief allies, examine the Lone Star and Axiom, choose a ship catalyzation, gather Spirit expertise, or establish evacuation and containment options.

**What makes it attractive:**

- The crew has just gained extraordinary equipment and has not evaluated it as a system.
- Axiom's Xal sense is particularly relevant to hidden boundaries, displaced spaces, and an Umbral breach.
- The Lone Star's one catalyzation can express what kind of instrument Asha wants her home and command to become.

**Core tension:** Preparation is useful only if tied to an identified risk. Catalyst hopping cannot substitute for choosing how to engage the crisis.

**A useful gain from this direction:** The party creates one concrete safeguard or diagnostic capability needed by another direction.

## Player significance

### Asha

Session 26 should make command—not piloting or combat—the source of her significance.

- She decides whether terrifying intelligence belongs to the whole crew or remains compartmentalized.
- She determines what “crew first” means when delay protects them but may endanger strangers.
- She has to maintain access to a man she despises without letting that relationship become ownership.
- The Lone Star's catalyzation can become a statement of doctrine if she chooses to make it relevant, but it should remain her choice.
- Her strongest contribution is defining acceptable risk and then committing the ship to it.

Useful pressure: Asha has repeatedly rejected being anyone's expendable asset. Now she controls information that could make other people into hers.

### Kaelith

Session 26 should connect his pursuit of magical understanding to stewardship rather than offer another simple increase in power.

- The Umbral mining operation is a direct violation of the Great Cycle he has tried to understand.
- His instinct to learn Spirit magic is useful, but it should raise the question of learning from whom and at what ethical cost.
- Axiom can distinguish boundaries, hidden spaces, and deliberate absences; it cannot yet explain Spirit, the Umbral, or the entire Spark system.
- His relationship with a nascent person gives him reason to notice how Maena turns souls, gods, and people into components.
- His strongest contribution is recognizing when an apparently complete magical model omits a whole category.

Useful pressure: Kaelith refuses to be a tool, yet the Hand has already used him as one and his first reaction is to use Axiom and Spirit knowledge against the enemy. The difference must become a practice, not merely an intention.

### The crew

If briefed, use the crew to widen the decision rather than vote on a correct answer.

- **Lysa** can press for understanding Spark's nature and challenge confident but incomplete magical claims.
- **Seraphine** can identify the military and political implications of touching Ternan production, and may recognize how a state hides a program without every official knowing.
- **Rho** can keep operational continuity, evacuation, and civilian fallout visible.
- **Tamsin** can turn abstract targets into questions about access, identification, and what “destroy it” actually means.
- **Yraina** alone carries the personal cost of using the Maena family connection. Her memory of how the Matriarch normally thinks may also be more useful than a routine magical scan: she can identify decisions that are politically plausible but personally wrong.

Do not make the supporting cast a council that decides for Asha and Kaelith. Their purpose is to reveal consequences the players can accept or reject.

## Axiom in Session 26

Current state after Xal:

- Pre-verbal and not independently spellcasting.
- Aware enough to make deliberate judgments.
- Able to sense hidden spaces, displacement, void pockets, and the shape of absent or severed structures.
- Still incomplete and liable to signal that another elemental principle is required.

Good uses:

- Recognize that a location or system contains a deliberately displaced component.
- Compare the boundary signature of the Hand's work, Maena's Spirit binding, and a later Umbral mechanism once evidence is present.
- Notice that the Matriarch's spiritual pattern contains a deliberate absence or concealed boundary even though Axiom cannot yet name it as a Nefarius/Necrotis binding.
- Indicate that a proposed plan ignores a hidden connection.
- Help define what must be isolated without explaining what lies on the other side.

Do not use Axiom to:

- locate the Trade Moon heart from the Flame or Lone Star;
- identify the Hand on demand;
- understand Spirit before the relevant catalyzation or instruction;
- speak fluently;
- certify one strategic route as correct.

## The Lone Star's catalyzation

Keep this optional. The threat provides a reason to revisit the decision, not a reason to force it.

If Asha chooses to catalyze now, frame the choice at the level of identity:

- Is the Lone Star meant to hide, endure, understand, move, rescue, or destroy?
- Is its single divine inheritance chosen for the present crisis or for the life Asha wants after it?
- Does she want a powerful tool, an eventual person, or a home whose nature will shape its crew?

Do not decide which Catalyst is optimal in advance. The consequence should express Asha's answer rather than invalidate it through a surprise “wrong choice.”

## High-level session architecture

### Movement 1: Contain the revelation

Give Asha and Kaelith space to react to Kek's final warning and ask only necessary clarifying questions. Kek may define terms he already used, distinguish an averaged pattern from a fact observed in this reality, or admit what he cannot see. If they push on Terra or accuse him of withholding, he may acknowledge that he has not been completely honest because what little he knows is too terrible to speak. He should not disclose that terror, supply a mission plan, provide target coordinates, or deliver a second campaign-scale revelation.

The movement changes when the players decide what information they are taking away and whether Nara is authorized to share it.

### Movement 2: Establish the circle of trust

The players decide whether to brief the crew, Aran, other Xal allies, Yraina, Rourke, Saezo, or nobody. Each inclusion should add capability and a viewpoint while creating a real exposure or obligation.

The movement changes when someone beyond the Flame bridge becomes a participant in the problem—or when the players explicitly choose isolation.

### Movement 3: Choose doctrine and first objective

Ask what they are trying to preserve, what they are willing to risk, and what they need to learn before decisive action. Use the modular directions as support, not menu text that NPCs read aloud.

The movement changes when the party can state an objective narrower than “stop Maena” and a constraint more concrete than “let Saezo believe he is in control.”

### Movement 4: Make the commitment real

If the choice comes early, begin the chosen direction at the level of contact, access, or first test. Do not require Session 26 to complete the operation. Deliver one consequence that proves the world is responding: a relationship accepts new terms, a cover story creates an obligation, a faction notices movement, or a diagnostic establishes a real dependency.

If deliberation fills the session, end when the crew commits resources and crosses a point after which secrecy, neutrality, or easy retreat is no longer available.

## Reveal budget

Session 25 was revelation-heavy. Session 26 should reward decisions with **leverage**, not another cosmology lecture.

Recommended maximum: one major operational fact and one character or faction truth.

Useful operational facts include:

- that Saezo's failure response is to dump the entire liquid reserve into the moon's deep Catalyst core;
- one separable dependency between the Spark reserve, pumps, engine, and core;
- evidence that Maena is preparing a political rather than military seizure of Saezo's network;
- one separable stage of Spark production;
- the continuing function of the Hand's corruption;
- one symptom or limitation of the mixed Nefarius/Necrotis binding.

Useful character or faction truths include:

- what Saezo thinks he is saving or building;
- what Yraina fears will happen if she returns to the family;
- what Rourke is protecting beyond his own authority;
- what price the Hand considers acceptable;
- what Nara will do if the crew chooses patience over assassination.
- whether the Matriarch recognizes any of the follower's desires as foreign to her own.

Do not reveal all of any category in one answer.

## Reveal constraints

Hold back until earned by the chosen direction:

- the exact physical and magical process that creates Spark;
- the full architecture of the Umbral mine or breach;
- the intended identity or personality of the unborn god;
- every safeguard, control route, and timing detail of Saezo's Spark dump before the party investigates the moon;
- the Hand's present membership, leader, base, and complete history;
- Soma's current condition or Terra's location;
- the Necrotis follower's identity, present form, and complete contingency structure;
- a simple detection spell that cleanly identifies or removes the Matriarch's mixed-blood binding;
- a numerical countdown to the end of possibility;
- a clean switch that safely ends the entire crisis.

The players already know enough to act. Mystery should now protect meaningful dependencies, not withhold every actionable fact.

## Consequence principles

- **Violence can work.** Its cost should arise from what the target was connected to, not from GM immunity.
- **Diplomacy can work.** Its cost should be obligation, exposure, or compromise, not an NPC refusing until the party chooses violence.
- **Deception can work.** Keep track of who now holds the false model and what happens when reality contradicts it.
- **Disclosure can work.** Give the new ally a real capability, then allow them to pursue their own interests with the information.
- **Delay can work.** It must produce preparation or understanding; otherwise interested factions continue their projects.
- **Splitting the party's focus can work.** The cost is coordination and information leakage, not arbitrary punishment for parallel action.

## Failure and redirection

Avoid binary dead ends.

- Failure to keep cover with Saezo should move his state from stable to suspicious before it becomes catastrophic, unless the players knowingly cross an established trigger.
- Failure to penetrate Ternan secrecy can reveal which institution is protecting the secret.
- Failure to contact the Hand can cause the Hand to respond indirectly, on its terms, with a demand or proof rather than an exposition dump.
- Failure to persuade a Maena insider can establish their loyalties and who they warn.
- Failure to read a magical system through Axiom should identify the missing elemental category rather than produce no information.

## Strong end states

Any of these can close Session 26 without dictating the route:

- The crew returns to the Trade Moon under a deliberately negotiated cover with Saezo.
- The Lone Star turns toward Ternus with a specific production question and a disclosure strategy for Rourke.
- Yraina agrees—or refuses—to reopen contact with the Matriarch, changing her relationship with the crew.
- The party creates a controlled signal or test intended to draw the Hand's attention.
- The crew accepts apparent anti-Maena work from Saezo, giving him a sense of control and themselves legitimate access to his infrastructure.
- The first sign of Maena's political succession effort is identified and quietly interrupted, delayed, or allowed to proceed under observation.
- A chosen Maena target is removed and the surviving network reveals its shape through its response.
- The Lone Star accepts its one catalyzation as an explicit statement of Asha's doctrine.
- The crew divides responsibility across two fronts and establishes how information will move between them.

The common requirement is a changed condition. “We will think about it” is not enough; someone has been told, something has been risked, a resource has been committed, or an interested faction has gained reason to react.

- Another direct use of the Web.
- Kek's succession or corruption arc.
- Cindara, the Cinder Hatchlings, and arachnidrake politics.

## Continuity risks

- Do not say the Hand of **Errata**; the established name is **Hand of Eterra**.
- Do not make Saezo the knowing architect. Kek called him a clueless pawn attempting to create a Catalyst.
- Do not make Saezo harmless. If he believes control is lost, he will dump the entire moon reserve into the deep Catalyst core and attempt to create a god.
- Do not treat “calm” as emotional comfort. Saezo is stabilized by believing that infrastructure, markets, subordinates, and rivals remain answerable to his leverage.
- Do not collapse “Maena” into a uniformly informed family. The Matriarch benefits from the program but is secretly bound; an old follower of Necrotis is the hidden operator; institutional knowledge still varies.
- Do not make the Matriarch innocent merely because she is bound. Her authority, gains, choices, and existing ambitions remain part of the problem.
- Do not let killing Saezo solve the problem by itself. His removal opens the political path for Maena to seize the contaminated reserve.
- Do not assume Rourke or Ternus leadership knows that Spark involves souls, dead gods, the Umbral, or the Trade Moon birth.
- Do not assume the entire crew knows Nara's prophecy or Kek's revelations. Asha and Kaelith were separated from them for both conversations.
- Do not return the Terra/Hand disk to Asha's inventory without retrieving it from Aran.
- Do not forget that the Hand's weapon is already in place and apparently protective. Destroying, cleansing, or replacing corrupted Spark may remove that protection.
- Do not treat Axiom as fully awake. It has a deliberate but pre-verbal Void baseline after one catalyzation.
- Do not conflate Axiom's eight-step catalyzation with the Lone Star's single catalyzation.
- Do not make the Web an unlimited source of answers because the group earned one audience.
- Do not treat Kek's averaged futures as exact surveillance of this branch. He is broadly correct and locally fallible.
- Do not let Kek explain Terra in Session 26. If pressed, his admission that he has withheld something is the reveal.
- Do not add a countdown unless play establishes a measurable process.

## GM decisions still required

### Parameters still required before play

The causal structure is now established. These decisions determine how the players can perceive and interact with it.

1. **What are the visible stages before Saezo's dump?** Define two or three preparatory actions—control lockdowns, pump rerouting, operator mobilization, core access, or political declarations—so loss of control produces warnings and possible intervention rather than an invisible catastrophe.
2. **Where can the reserve-to-core chain be interrupted?** Choose at least two dependencies with different costs. The reserve, pumps, deep engine, Catalyst core, and Saezo's command authority should not all be one binary target.
3. **What does Maena's political assassination look like?** Decide which legal, financial, reputational, or guild mechanism removes Saezo and how the actual killing or disappearance is concealed inside it.
4. **What is the Necrotis follower's current form and access?** Name, body, location, and public role can remain hidden from players, but the GM needs to know whether the operator acts beside the Matriarch, through Spirit, through a corporate proxy, or from the Umbral operation.
5. **What can expose the mixed binding?** Recommended layered answer: Void perception detects a deliberate concealment or missing boundary; Spirit expertise identifies coercion; a third principle such as Life, Dream, or personal memory separates the Matriarch's authentic desire from the implanted direction. No single routine scan should do all three.
6. **What does cracking the taint require?** Identify the research dependency the follower currently lacks. This creates something the party can deny, falsify, steal, or monitor.
7. **What little does Kek know about Terra?** This does not need to be revealed now, but his terror and refusal will be more convincing if the GM knows the image, fact, or implication he is protecting them from.

### Choose after the players declare direction

- Which single operational fact can that route reveal?
- Which faction or character truth makes the route personally costly?
- What action advances one pressure front?
- What success changes the players' leverage without ending the campaign problem?

## Current recommended emphasis

If no player preference emerges immediately, emphasize the **Trade Moon reserve-to-core chain** and **Matriarch/operator fault line** as the two most connected directions.

- The Trade Moon pays off the pumping station, patron, liquid reserve, and deep engine already established.
- Yraina's family connection makes the bound Matriarch personal and prevents the Necrotis follower from remaining a faceless explanation.
- Protecting Saezo from Maena while quietly disarming his god-engine creates a productive contradiction rather than a simple alliance or assassination target.
- Together they support infiltration, negotiation, investigation, or violence without prescribing any one of them.
- Ternus, the Hand, Axiom, and the Lone Star remain viable support routes rather than competing campaign resets.

Do not have an NPC choose these routes for the party. The emphasis should appear through available access and consequences, not instruction.
