# Inurans

The Inurans are seemingly everywhere throughout confederate space, but no one knows where they originate. Theories abound about their home world, but none have been confirmed. Fear of the Consortium prevents most parties from investigating too closely.

Inurans are divine in appearance, angelic in a way that fits many religions. They’re also cruel, and devious, exploiting their appearance whenever possible. The Consortium has a terrible reputation, and most Inurans do nothing to improve that.

The only thing despised more than an Inuran is a Drifter. Inurans, though, have far more power and influence due to the Consortium. Many people assume that any Inuran is part of the Consortium, making them far less likely to backstab or even question them. Why risk offending the most ruthless organization in the sector?

Not all Inurans are evil or manipulative, but enough are to permanently stain their race’s reputation in the cosmos. If this bothers them, the Inurans certainly don’t show it.

Despite refusing to reveal the location of their homeworld, the Inurans were formally adopted into the Shayan Confederacy as a full voting member. They are smaller than either the Shayan or Ternus factions, but possess more wealth than either.

### **Appearance**

Inurans have pale, creamy skin. Like Shayans, they possess a nearly supernatural beauty. Their hair color ranges across the same spectrum as humans, and they are generally indistinguishable from an attractive human. Inurans can (and often do) feed on magic, but must also sustain themselves by eating regularly. If an Inuran has fed on magic recently, they glow with a soft white halo of seemingly divine light. The color of this light doesn’t change based on the type of magic consumed.

### **Inurans & Magic**

Unlike Shayans, Inurans age normally and possess a similar lifespan to humans. That doesn’t mean they are without magic, quite the opposite. Inurans are famous for their enchanting, which requires both Life and Air magic. It is this natural affinity that led to the creation of the Consortium, and the source of the Inuran’s enormous success.

## Trade Moon

### Random notes

There's an Air reactor at the center.

There are signs which indicate which are they are in. A black area means you are responsible for your own security. If you report a crime, it will be logged, and then promptly ignored unless you have the credits.

An orange area has automated security installed which discourages most open violence. Snipers and the occasional explosives still get used, but that's rare unless someone is acting on a grudge. Destroying a turret will get you hunted down.

Surface of their moon is a lawless wasteland. If you aren't wealthy enough for security, you aren't wealthy enough to matter.

We want to treat the players to a rich and complex world on the trade moon. There are tram tubes, stairwells, elevators, all manner of transits. The buildings on the surface are generally extensions of underground structures - corporations and entities rich enough to buy a slice all the way to the surface. Almost all commerce is conducted deep below ground, many levels below the surface. Everything from gift shops to industrial shops, electronics markets, and all forms of magitech.

### Behind The Scenes

Right now, Spark is being traded in massive quantities, imported from Ternus in exchange for goods and credits. Referencing early session logs, Ternus was hit by an attack on its docks when Asha and Kaelith brought the Inuran ship to dock.

Saezo is secretly trying to turn that disaster into leverage. He is not a screaming villain twirling his mustache in public. He is the sort of Inuran who profits by staying two layers behind everything, which matches what was established about him in Session 10: war merchant, salvage interests, and a proxied board seat in the Merchant's Guild.

He does not need to have personally ordered the dock attack to be dangerous. In fact, it is better if he did not. The stronger version is that he recognized the opportunity immediately:

- Ternus lost face and infrastructure right before the Trade Moon's arrival.
- Rebuilding the docks forced Ternus into a weaker bargaining posture.
- Spark suddenly became even more important as a trade commodity and wartime lever.
- Anyone who can control shipping, salvage rights, and VIP trade access during the opening rush can become vastly more powerful.

Saezo wants Asha and Kaelith on the Trade Moon for multiple reasons at once:

- They survived the job tied to his original contract and the disaster that followed.
- They know pieces of the truth around the dock attack, the ship, and the cargo, even if they do not yet know the full picture.
- Asha is becoming publicly valuable. By Session 18 she and Kaelith are being shaped into folk heroes, which makes them useful to court, flatter, manipulate, or weaponize.
- Kaelith is tied to void lore, relics, and old magic in ways Saezo may not fully understand, but he is too important to ignore.
- Guild hospitality lets Saezo put them in a gilded cage. VIP treatment sounds generous, but it also means escorts, curated routes, monitored meetings, and carefully chosen witnesses.

Saezo's real goal is to learn three things before the opening trade frenzy fully peaks:

1. How much does the crew know about the original ship, the dock attack, and the forces behind both?
2. Can they be hired, steered, or bought before one of his rivals reaches them?
3. Are they a threat to his spark position, or a tool he can point at a rival guild bloc, Ternus official, or Consortium operator?

He should come across as intelligent, charming, and dangerous in a deeply civilized way. He is not trying to scare them first. He is trying to make them feel chosen.

One possible truth behind the scenes: the dock attack was not a simple attempt to kill Asha and Kaelith. It was a rushed move by another interested party trying to either recover something from the ship, destroy evidence tied to null/blood alloy and Spark, or destabilize Ternus right before Trade Moon negotiations. Saezo has spent the intervening time quietly identifying which rival faction moved first. He invited the crew because they are one of the last uncontrolled pieces on the board.

### How the Trade Moon should feel

The Trade Moon should feel like a miracle of wealth built on predation.

- Everything is beautiful, expensive, and quietly hostile.
- Inuran design language should lean angelic, immaculate, and subtly theatrical.
- Security is not about justice. It is about preserving profitable order.
- The deeper the crew goes, the more real the moon becomes. The upper layers sell prestige. The middle layers sell commerce. The lower layers sell what people are too rich or too compromised to admit they need.

Players should quickly understand that the Trade Moon is not one market. It is a vertical empire of markets, machine rooms, shrines to commerce, guild enclaves, smuggler corridors, and luxury spaces designed to make ordinary people feel temporary.

### Easy district structure

Use a few strong layers instead of too many named places.

#### 1. Surface Docks

No special landing privileges. The crew comes in with everyone else through the surface dock sprawl: freight towers, docking gantries, customs lanes, cargo shrines, transit queues, hired guns, bored officials, and too many ships stacked too close together. Saezo cannot sweep them straight into luxury. The best he can do is send an emissary called a personal shopper to meet them planetside and escort them below.

#### 2. Black Exchange Levels

Salvage rings, deniable brokers, questionable relic dealers, illicit enchanting, stolen manifests, hard information, and violence that only matters if someone important pays attention. This is where the crew can learn what people are not saying in the upper docks or the respectable markets below.

#### 3. Orange Market Levels

Legitimate commerce with automated security. This is where ship upgrades, spell armor, catalyst hardware, reagents, magitech components, and respectable artificers can be found.

#### 4. Coreward Infrastructure

Restricted transit, reactor-adjacent zones, freight arteries, maintenance sectors, and the places where the moon stops feeling like a city and starts feeling like a machine. This is where any catalyst-core or Spark conspiracy becomes tangible.

#### 5. Surface Spines

The moon's visible skyline and wasteland edge. Wealthy compounds, corporate towers, landing platforms, and dead open space where security becomes private and brutal.

### NPC pressure points to keep in play

- **Saezo Ekron:** Wants to charm, assess, and quietly own the first conversation.
- **Guild rivals:** Some will resent the VIP treatment immediately and assume Asha has already chosen a side.
- **Ternus representatives:** Desperate to turn Spark into long-term leverage before the quotas lock them in.
- **Drifters and black-market operators:** They will see the crew as unusually useful people who can move between classes and security zones.
- **Inuran enchanters / artificers:** Excellent sources of upgrades, bait, and soft coercion.

### Good opening pressure for the first session there

Give the crew three simultaneous truths as soon as they arrive:

1. They are being welcomed.
2. They are being watched.
3. They are part of a larger plot.

That keeps the VIP invitation attractive, threatening, and useful all at once.

### Landing intro scene

The goal of the intro is to make the Trade Moon feel vast, predatory, and socially legible before the players start pulling on threads. The first few minutes should communicate that Saezo has reach, but not sovereignty. He can send someone important to receive them. He cannot make the moon stop being itself.

#### Phase 1 - Final approach

The Trade Moon should not read as a city first. It should read as a machine-world wearing wealth like armor.

What they see:

- Surface docks stacked in harsh layers: gantries, freight towers, ship berths, cargo shrines, docking lights, tram spines, and traffic lanes packed far too tight.
- Too much movement for comfort. Ships coming and going, cargo elevators sliding through the outer skin, magitech cranes flexing under strain.
- Structural hints that the moon goes downward in layers rather than outward like a normal port.

What they hear:

- Dock control chatter in too many languages.
- Klaxons, cargo bells, loader impacts, warning horns, spell-crane resonance.
- A deeper constant hum under everything, like an industrial heartbeat.

What they smell:

- Hot metal, ozone, fuel, machine oil.
- Expensive incense and perfume trying to cover the dock stink and only partly succeeding.

What it communicates socially:

- This place values throughput more than comfort.
- Status matters, but not enough to save anyone from the line.
- If you have credits, reputation, or muscle, doors open.
- If you do not, the surface will eat you and invoice the remains.

#### Phase 2 - Touchdown in the surface docks

This is not a luxury arrival. It is a feeding ground.

What the players should notice immediately:

- Customs lanes that feel more performative than protective.
- Hired guns, haulers, port brokers, shrine-keepers, smugglers, pilgrims, rich travelers with escorts, poor travelers moving quickly and not making eye contact.
- Signage and district markers that quietly tell people what sort of violence is tolerated here.
- Eyes on the *Lone Star* almost immediately, especially if Asha and Kaelith are recognizable from circulating holos.

The lesson of the surface docks is simple: safety exists here, but it is rented.

#### Phase 3 - Yraina arrives

Yraina should stand out not because she makes a scene, but because the dock unconsciously gives way around her.

**Visuals**

- Inuran woman with the build of an Olympic athlete.
- Platinum blonde hair, gold eyes, stunningly beautiful in the unnerving Inuran way.
- Expensive clothes cut for movement rather than fragility.
- Every detail immaculate, but practical enough to survive a crowded dock.

**What she is doing beneath the smile**

- Confirming identities.
- Signaling to opportunists that this crew has been claimed.
- Taking the party's social temperature almost instantly.
- Beginning a curated escort on Saezo's behalf.
- Offering freedom of choice while quietly reducing chaos into routes she can manage.

**How to play her**

- Calm, warm, and never overeager.
- Efficient without sounding rushed.
- Slightly amused by chaos, never rattled by it.
- Polite in a way that still feels like containment.

Yraina is not a servant in the ordinary sense. A personal shopper on the Trade Moon is a concierge, guide, fixer, status marker, handler, and very polite form of surveillance.

### Yraina - GM character notes

Yraina speaks like every sentence has already been priced correctly.

She does not gush. She does not grovel. She offers service in a way that makes it sound completely natural that the moon should rearrange itself around the guest.

#### How she handles the crew

- **Asha:** Treat as the principal guest. Respectful, attentive, and flattering. She makes it clear Saezo's invitation was personal and quietly tests whether Asha enjoys status, distrusts it, or plans to weaponize it.
- **Kaelith:** Measured courtesy with an extra fraction of care. She reads him as potentially dangerous, important, or both. If he probes, she answers carefully. If he stays quiet, she tries to prod.
- **Seraphine:** Professional respect. Fewer flourishes, cleaner answers. If Seraphine asks about security, Yraina becomes briskly useful.
- **Rho:** Straightforward and practical. She gives route, timing, and threat answers cleanly because she will immediately clock him as the one checking exits.
- **Lysa:** Wary amusement. She recognizes curiosity and intelligence fast, and answers enough to reward sharp questions without surrendering control.
- **Tamsin:** Minimal fluff, maximum professionalism. Yraina will not crowd her and will not mistake silence for passivity.
- **Nyrix:** Exacting courtesy. No visible surprise. If Nyrix engages, Yraina treats her as someone important without trying to define what she is.

#### What Yraina knows going in

- Asha Morrow is the principal guest and should be handled personally.
- Kaelith matters.
- The crew is tied to the original contract and the dock disaster.
- Saezo wants them guided, observed, and given room to choose.
- Whatever else they are, they are not ordinary clients.

#### What Yraina does not do

- She does not lie casually if a clean omission will do.
- She does not over-explain Saezo.
- She does not pressure them to go only where Saezo wants.
- She does not break composure in public unless something has gone very wrong.

### Immediate choices once Yraina meets them

Give the players clear handles right away:

1. Follow Yraina below immediately.
2. Question Yraina at the docks before moving.
3. Ask to go straight to the **Black Exchange Levels**.
4. Ask to go straight to the **Orange Market Levels**.
5. Ask for secure lodging or a private room first.
6. Ask Yraina to arrange a scry-screen conversation with Saezo.
7. Spend time walking the surface docks before committing.
8. Test Yraina with misleading questions, faction probes, or split attention.
9. Ask which districts are most dangerous, most expensive, or most useful.
10. Split the party, with Yraina helping or refusing depending on the request.

### Easy scene progression after first contact

If you want a clean structure for the opening session:

1. **Approach and landing** - establish scale and mood.
2. **Surface dock pressure** - establish danger, class, and attention.
3. **Yraina introduction** - give them a face, a guide, and a social read.
4. **First choice** - let the players decide whether the session starts with risk, shopping, information, or controlled hospitality.
