You leave Lone Star Station with the sweet caramel stink of burned Spark still clinging to the back of your throat. The battleship is heavier now, full of new tanks and fresh warheads. You are still alive, and your streak of good luck continues to be on the upswing.

You have new weight on board besides cargo. Corvin Latch is in your bay, already measuring out the space so he can build it to his standards. Sergeant Daniel Calder is on your decks with her drone packed away, wearing that calm marine professionalism that says she has seen worse and lives for this shit. The Confederacy calls it a ride-along while the Governor of New Texas calls it hope. Either way, it is another set of eyes in your corridors, yet again. Perhaps this begins to grate on your nerves, or for some of you it might quietly kindle thoughts of becoming a legend.

You check seals. You check your people. Smoke runs drills with the tech mages and the marines until the ship hums like a well-oiled machine. You take one look at the map, filter out the obvious suicide options, and point your stolen city of steel toward a lesser threat.

Kumoshi: your air catalyst wrapped around a frozen planet, drifting far out where any traffic is thin and often aggressive. On the scale that matters, it is smaller than what you just survived. That does not mean it is safe. It just means it will not kill you quickly if you make one mistake. It will take its time.

The tech mages on board supply Asha with military-grade maps through the umbral depths in record time when she calls for it. Kaelith finds the R&R for their battleship has given new life to the engine and spell-drive; it doesn't pull so hard on his magic as he drops them into the Umbral Depths. The bridge lights dim slightly to indicate the ship has entered low-power mode. Lysa casts faint life warding around the ship to keep it sealed tight in case anything physically latches on.

You are going catalyst hunting again, this time with a plan, a bigger crew, and without all of your suspicions about the greater forces at play resolved.


The bridge is lit low, all practical glow and instrument wash. Displays float in clean layers across the forward stations, nav math crawling in pale lines while the jump solution finishes locking. The ship hum is steady, but it has that deeper undertone now, like the drive is awake and listening.

Rho has claimed the XO chair again, settled in like he belongs there, posture loose but eyes sharp. He is not looking at any one screen for long, just scanning, checking the room the way a veteran checks exits. Tamsin is off to the side with a datapad, two fingers pressed to her forehead like she is trying to keep a headache from turning into a prophecy. She scrolls, pauses, scrolls again, the kind of reading that looks like work even when it is silent.

Lysa stands near the open space by the tactical rail, practicing her step through invisible geometry. Small movements, precise, like she is training muscle memory for a fight that does not care how tired you are. She stops and starts again, breath controlled, eyes flicking to the forward glass between repetitions. Past them, Seraphine is at the viewport, still and unreadable, watching station light and distant traffic slide away as the battleship angles out. She does not speak. She does not need to. The room already knows what comes next.
