# Session 20 Scene Summary

## Scene 1 - Trade Moon Forge reset and shopping choices

### What happened

The session opened with a recap of the crew's Trade Moon shopping position. The group still needed to resolve Kaelith's future Eldimagus staff, quotes for the Lone Star's catalyzable retrofit, marine upgrade packages, and any remaining high-end gear. The GM presented the main options: buy now, quote now and install later, ask Yraina for special sourcing, push Saezo for advance payment, or skip to the freighter.

Kaelith chose to have Yraina find someone serious about an Eldimagus staff. Asha sorted through her new gear and realized carrying a hand cannon, spellrifle, and spellhammer all at once was cumbersome. Asha then had a memory of Aran and Nara using simple void pockets. She succeeded on Spellcasting, earned +1 EXP, and opened a void pocket for her spellhammer.

### Major points

- Enhancement rings are still tentatively capped at half the wearer's Magic stat.
- The crew has enough money to shop seriously, but the best purchases now carry political attention.
- Kaelith wants a world-changing Eldimagus staff capable of growing with every element of magic.
- Asha can now store at least one or two weapons in a void pocket, and she put her spellhammer there.
- Rho, Tamsin, and others bought/admired new gear and consumables offscreen.

### GM needs to remember

- Asha received +1 EXP for successfully learning the void pocket trick.
- Asha will likely always have her spellhammer available even when it is not visibly carried.
- The Lone Star retrofit and crew equipment upgrades were deferred, not abandoned.
- The players enjoy the Trade Moon as a place for grounded skill use, shopping, negotiation, and relationship scenes.

### Players need to remember

- Kaelith's staff is not just a purchase. It is now a major character/future-companion arc.
- Asha has a useful hidden-weapon trick.
- The crew still needs to handle marine upgrades and Lone Star catalyzation quotes later.

## Scene 2 - The Quiet Bench and Orrel Tain

### What happened

Yraina led Kaelith and Asha away from the polished Trade Moon markets to a hidden workshop called **Tain Salvage Calibration**, also known from prep as the Quiet Bench. The shop was a hot, dusty, practical workshop rather than a luxury showroom. Orrel Tain, a Ternan master artificer with a cybernetic eye and no patience for vanity, questioned Kaelith's understanding of what an Eldimagus is.

Kaelith initially described the staff as an extension of himself and a tool of great power. Orrel pushed back hard, insisting that a fully sentient Eldimagus is closer to a child or conscious being than a tool. Kaelith eventually answered that if the staff awoke and disagreed with him, he would try to teach it right from wrong and, if necessary, contain its power to prevent harm. Orrel accepted that answer enough to work with him.

Orrel explained that metamaterials form magical channels. The order of catalyzation matters because the staff must be built before being exposed to Catalysts; individual components cannot be catalyzed separately and later fused coherently. He proposed building around Kaelith's catalyst path and charged **285 million credits**, due in a few days.

### Major points

- Orrel Tain is obscure, not weak. He is good enough to charge absurd sums and refuse bad work.
- Orrel treats Eldimagi as future people, not trophies or stat sticks.
- Kaelith plans for the staff to eventually visit every elemental Catalyst.
- The staff should be catalyzed as a completed item in a deliberate order, not component by component.
- Asha offered to cover the difference if Kaelith could not meet the full price alone.

### GM needs to remember

- The exact quoted staff cost is **285 million credits**.
- Orrel said to return in a few days; he is in no hurry and will sell/build eventually if they do not.
- Orrel sent the invoice through Yraina.
- Kaelith's known catalyst sequence mentioned in the scene: Aashitak, Vaelith, Xal, Shaya. Orrel will likely design around a deliberate order rather than exactly repeating that order.
- Asha observed the exchange and may hold Kaelith to the "future child" framing.

### Players need to remember

- The staff cannot simply be assembled from pre-catalyzed parts.
- Orrel expects Kaelith to treat the staff as a future conscious companion.
- The staff price is enormous but within the party's combined reach if they choose to prioritize it.

## Scene 3 - Maena restricted ping and Yraina's new deal

### What happened

After leaving Orrel's shop, Yraina received a restricted ping connected to the staff invoice. The purchase was blocked at a high level by **Maena Biologic Interface Group**, effectively the Maena family. The restriction specifically referenced a **Spark-compatible neural lattice** and denied direct release.

The listed staff materials included:

- Bone of Xal
- Tears of Aashitak
- Crystal breath of Vaelith
- Blood of Shaya
- Gems of Wrathian
- Shell of Fiscarra
- Reliquary Glass of Necrotis
- Scales of Kee

Yraina explained that Maena is extremely powerful and effectively untouchable on Inura. Saezo would be an underdog against them but at least has a punching chance. She also admitted Saezo is onto something and hired her last-minute in a way that changed her career trajectory.

Asha asked whether Maena was "unfuckwithable." Yraina said yes if the crew draws the wrong level of attention, but also pointed out that the huge trade frenzy creates gaps. Yraina then proposed a new deal: take her along as crew, let her find a Maena lead and get them where they need to be, then pay her what they think she is worth at the end. Asha accepted in spirit, joking about not wanting to part with Yraina afterward.

### Major points

- Maena is now explicitly connected to Kaelith's staff problem.
- The phrase **Spark-compatible neural lattice** is a major clue.
- Yraina is no longer just a personal shopper; she is offering to become a temporary fixer/crew asset.
- Yraina is ambitious, risk-tolerant, and not offended by danger if the payoff is large enough.
- Kaelith raised the possibility that Yraina could be a Maena spy, but did not say he distrusted her.
- Asha's attitude toward Yraina is increasingly "crew adoption."

### GM needs to remember

- Maena's restriction should connect later to cybernetic spell interfaces, Spark fuel, and neural casting architecture.
- Yraina has not been proven loyal. Recommended state: sincere and ambitious, not a Maena plant by default.
- Yraina knows enough about Inuran power to be useful but not enough to fully understand Saezo's game.
- The under-the-table staff deal is still possible if Yraina and Orrel can bypass the restriction.
- Maena may notice the attempted purchase, especially if the crew pushes openly.

### Players need to remember

- Maena blocked the staff component, not Saezo.
- Yraina believes the crew can poke Maena if they stay below the wrong level of attention.
- Yraina is currently with the crew because of a negotiated risk/reward arrangement, not because she has sworn permanent loyalty.

## Scene 4 - Return to the Lone Star and crew reactions

### What happened

The crew returned to the Lone Star. Seraphine and Nyrix were on the bridge, occupying Rho and Asha's chairs. Asha introduced Yraina as their Trade Moon salesperson whom they had adopted as part of the crew. Yraina was visibly impressed by the cargo hold, the demons and half-demons, Seraphine, and especially Nyrix.

Rho joked that the job sounded easy: check the ship, head back, report, get paid. Lysa asked what they would do if they found something serious on a ship allegedly involved in an attack on Ternus. Asha said they had already told Saezo half-truths and could keep doing that depending on what they found.

Asha and Nyrix had a playful moment at the pilot's chair, with Nyrix pretending to be the pilot and enjoying Asha's admiration. The Lone Star then left the Trade Moon for Ternus on a priority trade lane.

### Major points

- Yraina has now seen that the Lone Star crew is much stranger than normal clients.
- Lysa raised the key evidence-handling question: if they find something serious, do they give it to Saezo?
- The crew is not fully committed to handing Saezo the whole truth.
- Nyrix and Asha continue to deepen their affectionate dynamic.
- The crew expected a short trip, but everyone lampshaded that jobs never stay simple.

### GM needs to remember

- Yraina should have opinions about the crew's weirdness next session; she has now seen enough to realize this is not a standard job.
- Lysa's question should remain active: who gets the evidence?
- Saezo's payment is still contingent on useful results.
- Asha may be inclined to keep Yraina if Yraina proves herself.

### Players need to remember

- They have discretion over how much truth they give Saezo.
- Yraina now knows more about the crew composition than most Trade Moon contacts.
- The freighter job is tied to both payment and larger political leverage.

## Scene 5 - Arrival at Saezo's secured Ternus dock

### What happened

The Lone Star traveled from the Trade Moon to Ternus through priority lanes. Ternus appeared as smog, metal, old damage, and fresh plating. Saezo's clearance routed them away from public traffic to a secured private dock.

The old Inuran freighter waited there: matte black hull, faded Inuran markings, old stealth coating, patched and sealed. The narration recalled the early campaign events: Asha towing the ship, Kaelith boarding from the Crowfeather with Joren and Tessa, Kaelith finding the armory, dream scale, armor, and Joren on the bridge. The dock itself had been rebuilt around the disaster, with extra scanners, marines, pressure gates, and evidence of the purple blast that tore kilometers of Ternus dock into open space.

Everyone who wanted to came along. Asha framed this as returning to where the odyssey started. Kaelith eagerly led toward the bridge and activated Essence Sight.

### Major points

- The old freighter is physically intact enough to investigate.
- The dock has been rebuilt but still carries the weight of the original disaster.
- The location is emotionally important: Asha and Kaelith first met after this incident.
- Saezo has enough pull to get the crew private access.
- The whole crew may be present unless later narrowed.

### GM needs to remember

- The freighter should feel like a returning origin point, not just a dungeon.
- The dock is under Saezo's authorization but still on Ternus, so Ternus security and political pressure can intrude.
- The ship's survival is suspicious and should continue to bother Rho, Seraphine, and the players.

### Players need to remember

- This is the same ship where the campaign's first major disaster began.
- The ship is tied to the dream scale, Nefarius armor, Joren's death, and the Ternus dock attack.
- Saezo's access does not mean the site is safe or private from every interested party.

## Scene 6 - Freighter bridge investigation and impossible magic

### What happened

Inside the ship, Kaelith's Essence Sight revealed old layered spellwork: Inuran lines under the hull, faded Dream around the cargo spine, Void in deeper seams and storage geometry, Fire in old weapon routes, and dormant war-era magic. On the bridge, the two magical matrices remained recessed in the deck, locked by Ternus restraint clamps. One ring had a hairline fracture filled with glassy purple residue, and Joren's evidence outline marked where he had touched the ring when the spell fired.

Kaelith saw a thin signature clinging to the matrix: not a proper spell, more like spellwork broken into pieces. It appeared blue-white and sterile silver, unlike anything he had seen.

Asha asked her armor what it felt. The armor said the vessel reeks of Nefarius, but that Nefarius is only a coating on something worse, something like glass that it cannot touch. Asha and Kaelith discussed whether Talifax could be involved. Kaelith's history roll suggested this was not Talifax's style; Eterra and Zenzuata from Aashitak's vision may be involved, but the answers are likely hidden in Ternus archives.

Sargoth said he felt dread and familiarity, and that Xal and Aashitak were connected to this place, perhaps through a great tragedy. Lysa questioned whether the crew's catalyst path had been coincidence or manipulated destiny. Asha recalled the sense of transcendent time/knowledge from both Xal and Aashitak.

Nyrix revealed that the creators/titans made the universe, that Xal was one of them, and that dragons ate them. She personally remembers eating them and saw Virkonna there as a smaller hatchling feeding on Xal's flesh. Kumoshi believes the magic here is Eterra's work, but is preventing Nyrix from seeing memories about Eterra. Nyrix said Eterra was the best of them and acted to stop something that threatened everyone.

Asha inspected the matrix ring more closely. The old spell was wrong, ancient, engineered, and not built from readable runes, language, or normal magical concepts.

### Major points

- The freighter contains old Dream, Void, Fire, and Inuran war magic, plus a newer/stranger blue-white sterile-silver signature.
- The bridge effect was not ordinary spellcasting.
- Asha's armor identifies Nefarius as a surface taint, not the deepest thing present.
- Sargoth connects the ship to Xal and Aashitak.
- Kaelith suspects the Ternus archives may reveal more about Eterra and Zenzuata.
- Nyrix dropped major cosmology: dragons ate the creators/titans; Xal was one; Virkonna was present; Kumoshi is blocking Eterra memories.
- Eterra may have acted to stop a threat to everyone.

### GM needs to remember

- The blue-white / sterile-silver signature is the key visual clue to connect freighter, Maena, staff restriction, and future Trade Moon scenes.
- The armor's "Nefarius coating over something worse like glass" should not be forgotten; this implies a deeper ancient layer.
- Talifax is probably not the answer here.
- Eterra and Zenzuata should be seeded through archives, protocols, visions, and Maena/Kemet records rather than fully explained immediately.
- Nyrix's cosmology reveal is huge and should have consequences in how the crew treats her knowledge and vulnerability.

### Players need to remember

- This ship is not just a Maena crime scene; it is tied to much older god/titan history.
- Nefarius may be involved only indirectly or as residue.
- Eterra and Zenzuata are the next names to research.
- Ternus archives are a live lead.

## Scene 7 - Seraphine finds the auxiliary data core

### What happened

While the crew debated the implications of the bridge magic, Seraphine quietly worked on a wall panel near the pilot's chair. She removed an auxiliary data core from behind it and commented that investigators had been lazy. She said they could find out more from it later.

Seraphine also argued that the attack was verifiably weak. If the enemy struck and it seemed underwhelming, that implied they had not yet understood where the real damage was or what the system was meant to do. Sargoth agreed: the attack was weak.

Kaelith said they needed to inspect the rest of the ship. Sargoth suggested a quick flame read could find the previous occupants. The session ended there because the player had to leave. Both players took 5 EXP.

### Major points

- Seraphine recovered an auxiliary data core from the bridge.
- Ternus investigators missed or ignored an important hidden recorder.
- The dock blast was weak relative to what the ship or spell might have been capable of.
- A flame read is an immediate next-session option.
- Kaelith wants to inspect every inch of the ship.
- Both players received 5 EXP at session end.

### GM needs to remember

- Start Session 21 on the freighter bridge with the aux core in hand.
- The immediate decision points are: read the aux core, flame read, search the bridge/ship, question dock staff, or head to archives.
- The aux core should contain enough to point toward Kemet, Maena, Spark-compatible neural interfaces, and/or recent Trade Moon access.
- The "weak attack" idea is important: the dock blast may have been a failed activation, degraded packet, test discharge, or side effect of a much larger system.

### Players need to remember

- They have a physical data core to examine.
- They have not finished searching the freighter.
- Sargoth suggested flame reading previous occupants.
- The disaster that changed their lives may have been a small symptom of something much larger.
