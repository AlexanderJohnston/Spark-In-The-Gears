# Session 25 Scratchpad

## Current scope

Plan only the opening movement of Session 25:

1. Reorient at the exact Session 24 endpoint.
2. Get Ikadra's blessing and catalyze Axiom through Xal.
3. Visit the Flame of Knowledge and consult the being who uses the Web of Divinity.
4. Deliver one bounded opening revelation.
5. Present the broader decision created by that revelation.
6. Stop planning when the players begin making that decision.

Do not plan the resulting operation, destination, encounter, or faction response yet.

## Early-session thesis

**Session 25 should turn foreknowledge into responsibility.**

The opening begins with a promised increase in power and immediately asks what that power is for. Axiom's first catalyzation should emphasize stewardship of a potential person. The Web consultation should then give the crew one actionable truth without solving Spark, Maena, Terra, Soma, or the coming catastrophe.

The desired movement is:

**power accepted -> question chosen -> partial truth learned -> responsibility chosen**

- In the futures where Asha and Kaelith die on the Trade Moon, every human soul on Ternus is subsequently pulled into the moon.
- A new God is born without first living.
- Possibility then ends: divination leads into a hole rather than branching futures.
- When the crew takes the freighter, tries to dock it, escapes with the scale, and survives, possibility opens again.

- It should have occurred shortly after the crew first reached the Trade Moon. Nara cannot explain why it remains delayed.

- The freighter's destruction at the dock is a consistent feature of the surviving timelines.

- Nara describes Spark as raw magic and does not know how it is bottled.

## Opening module 1: Ikadra's blessing

### Function

Frame Axiom as a future person before Kaelith changes it permanently.

### What this beat needs to establish

- Ikadra's blessing is informed recognition from one Eldimagus to another, not a magical permission slip.
- Kaelith understands that catalyzation is inheritance and formation, not simply upgrading equipment.
- Voria can advise without taking ownership of Kaelith's choice.

### Useful questions for Kaelith

- Is he raising a companion, making a successor, or building a tool?

- Do not make Ikadra solemn merely because the moment is important; its established irreverence can coexist with serious judgment.
- Do not have Voria grant, own, or control Axiom.
- Do not awaken Axiom fully or give it an extensive ability suite.

- a boundary has been cut, sealed, or displaced;
- a question is missing a necessary category;

## Transition: going to the Flame of Knowledge

### Location continuity

The new location guide describes the Flame's historical/default location as deep in the wreckage of the Vagrant Fleet near Kemet. Campaign logs from Sessions 13-14, however, placed the Flame beside the Skull of Xal. Session 24 does not explicitly confirm whether it is still parked there.

For this campaign, logged table history takes priority over the general location summary. Before play, decide whether:

- the Flame remains beside the Skull and can be reached immediately;
- Aran translocates the party to wherever it currently rests; or
- the earlier sighting was temporary and reaching it is a later undertaking.

Because the requested scope requires the Web revelation in the opening, the cleanest current assumption is that Xal's faction can provide immediate controlled access without running the Vagrant Fleet expedition structure.

### What must be clear

- The Flame is a derelict, fire-aspected Great Ship at least ten thousand years old, not a functioning conventional library ship.
- The Web is aboard the **Flame of Knowledge**, linked to its bridge and temporal matrix.
- Aran warned that visiting the Flame is dangerous.
- The Archive occupies much of the vessel and stores ancient knowledge on inscribed dragon scales.
- The Swarm has webbed off the approach to the ship's bridge, not merely a walkway inside the library.
- It normally attempts to destroy or drive away anything approaching the defended bridge.
- The Web hangs above a fire Catalyst well and interfaces with the bridge's temporal machinery.
- Speaking to the Swarm is different from directly entering the temporal matrix and using the Web.
- Even a single direct encounter with the Web eventually inflicts permanent insanity. Asha and Kaelith should not be casually offered direct access.
- The Flame also contains or channels a trapped darkness that may notice a user through the opening made by temporal perception.
- The purpose of the visit is to ask a bounded question through the being already carrying that damage, without crossing the bridge or connecting a PC.

Do not make travel into a Vagrant Fleet expedition in this early-session plan. The important threshold is whether Nara can secure a controlled audience and whether the Swarm chooses to mediate the question. If it refuses, stop rather than converting the opener into an eight-zone dungeon.

### Canon facts held in reserve

## The Swarm: user and guardian of the Web

### Confirmed nature

The Swarm is an ancient entity spread throughout the Flame of Knowledge, especially the Archive and the webbed approach to the bridge. It is not a conventional colony. Millions or billions of spiders share one intelligence and move as the countless bodies of a single distributed creature.

- Most of its bodies are ordinary spiders or spiders ranging up to approximately small-dog size.
- Some bodies are human-sized and partly humanoid, with hands suited to physical work and interfaces.
- It can assemble its bodies into temporary macroscopic structures, including enormous arachnid faces, speaking forms, or long prehensile limbs.
- It can communicate without ordinary speech through coordinated movement and vibration across its webbing, but can also form structures capable of speech when it chooses.
- All bodies express the same collective consciousness. They are not distinct speakers or personalities.
- The records suggest that it was created to tend the Web and Archive. Its deeper origin and creator remain uncertain.
- Millennia of Web exposure and the Flame's trapped darkness have corrupted caretaking into violent quarantine.
- It has webbed off the bridge and normally attempts to destroy or drive away intruders.
- It can form prehensile masses powerful enough to threaten ships; it is the vessel's surveillance system, architecture, and pursuing antagonist at once.
- Increasing web density warns that visitors are approaching a defended region.

### Interpretive caution

- Do not split the Swarm's madness among competing personalities. Contradiction, fixation, and damaged reasoning all belong to one mind.
- Do not assume its original created purpose proves that its present service is voluntary, involuntary, or unchanged.
- Do not treat its violence as mindless hostility. Guarding the bridge is an ancient purpose expressed through corruption, paranoia, and damaged intent.
- Do not require the party to cross the bridge merely to ask the earned question. The Swarm can mediate the Web itself if it accepts the inquiry.

### Interaction bounds

- The Web presents branching possibilities rather than a single prophecy. The Swarm can select and mediate a constrained comparison.
- It does not know everything that exists outside the Web.
- The Web's images may be accurate while the Swarm's selection and interpretation remain damaged.
- The Swarm can describe stable differences more reliably than ultimate causes.
- Separate direct Web observation, Swarm interpretation, and GM inference.
- Its madness can distort sequence, emphasis, identity, or certainty, but should not turn the exchange into random nonsense.
- Its first priority is guarding the Web and Archive, not answering visitors.
- Coordinated stillness, retreat, enclosure, or a single shudder through the web can communicate as clearly as a constructed speaking face.
- It may answer by arranging bodies into symbols or text, forming a face, or forcing several possible scenes to overlap through the temporal machinery.

### Why a consultation is possible at all

Nara's Session 24 wording—"you can speak to the one who does use it"—implies that Xal's faction has some known means of approaching or communicating with the Swarm. It does not prove obedience, friendship, or safe access.

Recommended opener assumption: Nara can obtain a guarded audience at the webbed threshold, but cannot command an answer. The Swarm agrees only because the question concerns the impossible hole in possibility that threatens the integrity of the Web itself.

## The question

### Player-earned question

**What would have happened if Kaelith had activated the freighter system instead of Joren?**

Let Asha and Kaelith phrase it themselves. If they broaden it into "what causes everything?", the operator should ask them to choose a narrower comparison. The useful comparison is:

- actual line: Joren activates, dock is destroyed, crew escapes with scale;
- counterfactual line: Kaelith activates with Void and Fire available.

### Question budget

Recommended default: one deep comparison, with clarification permitted but no second unrelated divination. The limit arises from interpretive damage, the Swarm's hostility, the darkness risk, and the strain of separating overlapping branches—not a game-like fee or arbitrary refusal.

## Opening revelation

The following are **GM proposals**, not established canon. Choose one before play.

### Recommended: the wrong activator completed the interruption

The Web shows that Kaelith's activation would have allowed the freighter system to complete a magical recognition or calibration that Joren could not supply. In that counterfactual, the dock does not fail in the same way and the future collapses rapidly toward the birth on the Trade Moon.

In the actual line, Joren's lack of innate magic causes an incomplete activation. The resulting dock catastrophe destroys or severs one necessary receiver, template, or connection. That damage buys time.

What this confirms:

- Kaelith would have made the outcome worse, not prevented it.
- Joren's apparent inadequacy as a trigger is part of why the crew survived.
- The dock explosion mattered causally.
- The delay rests on something damaged or missing, not on destiny permanently changing.

What this does **not** confirm:

- what the system ultimately is;
- who designed it;
- whether Maena understood its full purpose;
- how Spark is made;
- why all Ternan souls are involved;
- what must replace the destroyed connection;
- how long the interruption lasts.

### Alternate A: the missing condition

The Web identifies one specific condition that was absent from the real dock event but present when Kaelith activates. The condition can be magical recognition, a viable living pattern, or the correct combination of elements. The freighter deviation prevented it without revealing the larger machine.

This is the most mystery-preserving version. It produces a decision about protecting the missing condition versus risking investigation of it.

### Alternate B: borrowed time is being corrected

The Web confirms that the freighter disaster only delayed the catastrophe and reveals one observable sign that an unknown actor or system is rebuilding around the interruption. It cannot identify the actor, mechanism, or timetable.

This creates the strongest immediate pressure, but it introduces new offscreen movement. Use it only if the GM wants the early decision to feel urgent rather than contemplative.

## How to present the revelation without over-answering

- Show a contrast between the two lines rather than a complete causal history.
- Give one stable difference and one stable consequence.
- Let Axiom react to the missing boundary or connection, reinforcing its new diagnostic identity without interpreting the whole vision.
- Let the Web show several variants before the Swarm isolates the one stable distinction.
- Make it explicit which images are Web observation and which claims are the Swarm's inference.
- Permit the players to challenge the interpretation.
- Do not attach a numerical countdown.
- Do not let the trapped darkness speak, identify itself, or escape merely because the Web is consulted. Its pressure should remain a risk and constraint.

## The broader decision

Stop detailed planning when the revelation places these responsibilities in front of the crew:

1. **Protect the interruption.** Treat the destroyed/missing condition as the immediate safeguard and prevent anyone from restoring it.
2. **Understand the mechanism.** Risk approaching the freighter, dock, Spark network, or associated records to learn what was interrupted.
3. **Find the architect.** Pursue the people who arranged the binding and delivery without assuming their ultimate knowledge or motive.
4. **Share or contain the warning.** Decide whether Xal's circle alone holds this truth or whether Ternus, Yraina, Saezo, or another party must be involved.

These are decision categories, not prepared routes. Let the players combine, reject, or reframe them. End this scratch pad's planned material once they establish their first priority and who they trust with it.
