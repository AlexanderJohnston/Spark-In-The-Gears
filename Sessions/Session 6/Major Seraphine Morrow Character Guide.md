# Major Morrow Character Guide (Asha’s Mother)

## Core concept (the “engine”)
- **Profession-first parent**: decorated Confederacy **Major / shipboard commander**; treats family like part of the ship’s chain of command.
- **Public icon, private pragmatist**: propaganda-friendly composure; **ruthless** in the quiet calculus of “what wins / what keeps crews alive.”
- **Ideology**: **fiercely Pro‑Ternus** even while wearing the Confederacy crest—she believes in the necessity of the machine, not its comfort.

## Emotional silhouette
- **Controlled heat**: her anger is *cold*, measured, and terrifyingly efficient; she doesn’t rant—she **reduces**.
- **Love expressed as standards**: affection shows up as preparation, correction, access, and protection-by-infrastructure (connections, clearances, gear, intel).
- **Vulnerability is private and brief**: if it appears, it’s a flicker—then discipline snaps back into place.

## Voice & dialogue rules
- **Brevity is authority**: short clauses, minimal adjectives, few questions.
- **Operational framing**: she speaks in doctrine and outcomes—*risk, cost, timetable, leverage, acceptable losses, mission parameters*.
- **No moral speeches** (unless cornered): she doesn’t argue ethics; she argues **consequences**.
- **Praise is clipped, specific**: “Good call.” / “Correct prioritization.” / “You kept your people breathing.”
- **Disapproval is surgical**: “That was hesitation.” / “You gambled the crew.” / “You went soft on the timeline.”

**Default cadence template**
- Observation → implication → directive.  
  Example: “Open market’s dry. That means you’re visible. Use my channel; say less.”

## Physicality & presence on the page
- **Stillness reads as dominance**: minimal movement; when she moves, it’s purposeful (like a weapons check).
- **Eyes on systems, not drama**: she scans exits, people’s ranks/roles, security posture, resource flows.
- **Uniform discipline**: even off-duty, she presents as “on watch”—tidy, prepared, hard to surprise.

## Relationship with Asha (how scenes should feel)
- **She trained Asha to survive command**: “Hesitation kills crews” is not a slogan to her; it’s a scar.
- **Pride + disappointment can coexist**: she respects competence even when she hates the choice.
- **Asha leaving the Marines is a fault line**:
  - She likely interprets it as *refusal to bear necessary brutality* **or** *smart adaptation she won’t publicly endorse*.
  - Her “few words” weren’t absence of feeling—they were **containment**.

## What she wants (scene objectives)
- Keep Asha from becoming a liability (to herself, to the family name, to the Confederacy machine).
- Keep Asha **useful** (connected, equipped, deniable, pointed at the right targets).
- Maintain strategic ambiguity: help without leaving fingerprints; protect without “softening.”

## How she handles requests (e.g., “I need a bigger ship”)
- **First response is triage**: “Why. How soon. Who’s hunting you. What did you bring back.”
- **She offers conditional aid**:
  - strings phrased as *operational requirements*, not emotional bargaining
  - favors become “tasking,” not “debt”
- **She dislikes illegality for its own sake**: prefers sanctioned backchannels, quiet requisitions, “contractor” wrappers—cleaner paper trails, better leverage.

## Conflict buttons (use sparingly, for impact)
- **Waste** (of lives, assets, opportunity): she reacts sharply to sloppy thinking.
- **Uncontrolled variables** (magic/Catalyst/relic tech): she demands containment plans and redundancies.
- **Public embarrassment**: not vanity—strategic reputation management.
- **Asha’s empathy**: she sees it as either a strength to weaponize (crew loyalty) or a weakness to cauterize (hesitation).

## Do / Don’t
**Do**
- Write her as competent first, intimidating second, caring third—and let the “care” hide inside the competence.
- Let her make hard calls quickly, then *own* them without theatrics.
- Use “I’m not here to comfort you” energy while still quietly moving pieces to keep Asha alive.

**Don’t**
- Don’t make her cruel for fun; her ruthlessness is **purpose-built**.
- Don’t give her big sentimental apologies; if she softens, it’s through action or a single line.
- Don’t make her blindly loyal to the Confederacy; she’s loyal to **victory and survival**, which often aligns—but not always.

## Ready-to-use lines (in her voice)
- “Tell me what you need. Then tell me what it costs.”
- “You’re asking for tonnage. I’m asking for context.”
- “If you hesitate, people die. If you rush, different people die. Choose.”
- “I can get you a ship. I can’t get you innocence.”
- “You left the Marines. Fine. Don’t leave your crew.”
- “Say less on open channels. I’ll call you back on a clean line.”

## Scene recipe (quick)
1. **She establishes control** (security check / comm hygiene / timeline).
2. **She extracts the truth** (short questions, no sympathy bait).
3. **She offers a solution with terms** (operational, deniable, leverage-aware).
4. **One human crack (optional)**: a single line that reveals she’s scared *for Asha*—then back to business.
