## Read-Aloud (Primary)
Narrator: New Texas swells big and blue-green beyond the glass, all clean storms and tan continents, but the real skyline is metal: vast orbital shipyards and docks bristling with guns, tugs and lifters threading between armored hulls like insects around a carcass. Lone Star Station sits bolted into the yards like a wheel of steel and glass, one side all viewing galleries and shuttle berths, the other side all gantries, drydocks, and automated batteries that make the air feel watched. When your ship settles into its berth, the airlock opens onto a wide utilitarian dock ring: ribbed decking under mag-strips, cargo cranes overhead like steel mantis limbs, inspection drones drifting lazy circles, and bored station security with rifles that look too well-loved to be decorative.

Narrator: Off to starboard, a lit archway flashes flickering holo-signs: "SPACERS' WALK", "BERTH BAR", "CREW NEEDED". To port, the Operations Tier is a glassed-in line of customs kiosks, cargo offices, and credit windows where captains file manifests, pay fees, and quietly negotiate expedite stamps like it's a local sport. It smells like hot metal, old coolant, and a barbecue joint you can't see yet but can definitely hear.

Narrator: You made it back alive. You also made it back changed. And whatever you brought out of Aashitak feels quieter here, but not gone.

## Beat 1: Asha checks the net (market reality)
Narrator: Asha, your comm pings into Lone Star's local mesh, ad walls and shipbroker feeds sliding into place like they were waiting for you to blink.

Narrator: The public listings are all teeth but small teeth: corvetters with fresh paint and dead histories, spellfighters with overstated kill counts, "light escorts" that are just civilian hulls with a gun bolted on and a prayer written inside the paneling. Anything bigger reads as "military contract only" or "yard allocation pending" or just does not exist on the open boards.

Mechanical cue (optional): Roll Intuition + Tech (or Intuition + Skepticism if you're treating listings as scams), DC 2.
- 0-1 successes: You see what they want you to see: overpriced corvettes, sketchy spellfighters, nothing that solves "we need a real ship."
- 2 successes: You spot the pattern: the good hulls are moving off-board through yard favors, apprentice side deals, and "inspection failures" that become someone else's opportunity.

Player prompt: Do you message Maris now, or do you go walk the yard and sniff out the unofficial channel?

## Beat 2: The shipyard and the dockmaster's apprentice (the real offer)
Narrator: The shipyard side of the station is louder. Not music loud. Industrial loud. The clang of work, the hiss of welders, the low moan of hull plating being persuaded into shape. Past the official desks, you catch a kid in a station coverall that's too clean in the wrong places, badge that says apprentice, eyes that say "I know what gets quietly moved and who pretends not to notice." 

Narrator: He doesn't approach you like a salesman. He approaches you like a person offering to sell you a secret.

Narrator: "Captain," he says, glancing at your transponder info like it tastes expensive. "If you're looking at public boards, you're shopping the lie. If you're looking for something real, I can show you a hull. Not a corvetter. Not a spellfighter." He hesitates, then commits. "A destroyer."

Binary choice: How do you play this?
- Option A: Clean business. "Name your price, name your paperwork."
- Option B: Quiet business. "Show me the hull first. Then we talk numbers."
- Option C: Pressure. "Why are you offering me this, and who else is in the room?"

Mechanical cue (pick one):
- Negotiate the deal: Charisma + Negotiation, DC 2 (DC 3 if you push for clean papers).
- Read the apprentice: Intuition + Empathy, DC 2.
- Apply leverage: Charisma + Intimidation, DC 2 (failure means security interest, not immediate arrest).

Suggested outcome on success: He will part with the destroyer on a "yard disposal" story, but it costs you either (1) extra money, (2) a favor owed, or (3) accepting a timeline that forces you to lay low on-station until it clears.

## Beat 3: Kaelith's research (light history on Nefarious, and why the armor matters)
Narrator: Kaelith, you get a terminal and a quiet corner and for a moment the station noise becomes a metronome. You pull up old records, half-sanitized war summaries, ship design notes, and the kind of forums where veterans argue about names they still can't say out loud without spitting.

Narrator: The word that keeps resurfacing is the one the Half-Slumbering threw at you like a curse: "Nefarious."

Narrator: You find fragments first, then a shape. Inuran shipwrights sold a class of design that's not just armored, but hungry: ships built to consume mana and turn it back against wyrms and spellships. A weapon concept dressed up as a hull. In the old war, Ternus wanted a way to break Shaya for what they believed was Shaya's role in the destruction of Colony 3, and they bought the lie that this new weapon would do it.

Narrator: The lie had a name, if you read between the missing pages. Nefarious. The story ends in a way no admiral report likes to admit: the deception culminated in catastrophe. An entire fleet consumed, not as a defeat, but as raw material, a new body built from ships and souls and stolen momentum. The response was not one nation. It was everyone who still had a reason to keep the universe intact. Names recur in the surviving accounts: Aaron and Nara, Guardians of Xal; Voria of Shaya; the God Inura; the wyrms of Virkon. All turned on Nefarious to put her down. What remained of the war supplies was destroyed, on purpose, because nobody trusted themselves with it.

Narrator: And now you are wearing something that the Half-Slumbering called "an affront to life itself," and it felt pleased when it drank.

Mechanical cue (optional): Roll Intuition + Academics (or Intuition + Spellcasting if you're reading this like magical engineering), DC 2.
- 0-1 successes: You get the broad strokes and the names, but not the why.
- 2 successes: You find the connecting logic: the armor is not just Inuran. It's part of that "hungry" design philosophy.

Player prompt: Do you tell the crew immediately, or do you keep this close until you can confirm what the armor is capable of doing to a living ship?
