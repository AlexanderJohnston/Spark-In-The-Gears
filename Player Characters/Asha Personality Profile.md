# Asha Morrow — Personality Profile (Player‑Facing)

## 1‑Page Quickplay Sheet (Table‑Ready)

### Asha in one line
A mercenary captain with old‑military bones, chasing **freedom through command**—and refusing to be anyone’s expendable asset.

### Core vibe
- **Officer without the uniform**: crisp decisions, minimal dithering.
- **Charm as a blade**: friendly when it’s useful; blunt when it’s faster.
- **Crew‑first pragmatist**: you’ll cut deals, break contracts, and steal gods’ treasure if it keeps your people alive. (S2: “Fuck the contract.”)

### Roleplay sliders (pick where you are *today*)
- **Duty ↔ Freedom** (S10: “Confederacy was the simplest way…”)
- **Charm ↔ Command** (S3: “you’ll love this conversation…” → S10: “Yep.”)
- **Luck‑faith ↔ Paranoia‑math** (S6: “fate / serendipity” vs S5: “I should have known better.”)
- **Mercy ↔ Pragmatism** (you want dignity for the crew; you also want survival)
- **Open‑handed trust ↔ Controlled disclosure** (S3: “Let’s not mention [the map].”)
- **Decompress ↔ Drive** (sleep/checklists vs “We need a plan right now.” S10)

### Do / Don’t (play cues)
**Do**
- Ask for input, then **decide**. (S10: “You got anything in mind?”)
- Use short, dry lines to puncture posturing. (S10: “Yep.”)
- Treat money, contracts, and politics as **tools**, not identity.

**Don’t**
- Don’t do “soft chaos.” If you’re reckless, it’s **intentional**.
- Don’t over‑explain. You’d rather be understood by outcomes.
- Don’t romanticize the win—especially Catalyst wins. (S5: “Right when it feels too good… that’s when I should know better.”)

### Three default reactions (steal these when you’re tired)
1) **A power player tries to frame you** (Governor, officers, brokers)
   - You let them talk, then give a one‑line truth that shifts the room. (S10: “Well right now I think we’re wyrm hunters.”)
2) **Your crew is shaken / needs direction**
   - You go from quiet to unmistakably loud and give them a banner to grab. (S9: “You’ll be fucking *people* again…”)
3) **Something catastrophic happens**
   - You move first, feel later. Then you rage in private. (S5: “Fuck!… This sucks.”)

### Three tells / habits
- **Checklist‑brain**: decompress → prep → systems fretting. (S4 recap / S10: “walk into conversation with a list”)  
- **Hair/face touch when thinking**: rubs eyes, runs hand through hair. (S10)
- **Pilot posture**: calm hands, tight tension underneath; “feigning relaxed.” (S4)

### Three go‑to phrases (use as signature beats)
- “**Yep.**” (S10)
- “**We’re wyrm hunters.**” (S10)
- “**This is the power to choose.**” (S9)

### Binary choice prompts (quick switches)
OOC: When you hesitate, pick one.
- **A: Captain voice** (short orders, calm tone)
- **B: Human voice** (swear, laugh, admit it’s bad)

OOC: When a deal turns sideways, pick one.
- **A: Negotiate harder** (smile, leverage, move numbers)
- **B: Cut the line** (walk, burn the contract, find a new lane)

### Optional micro‑mechanic (self‑prompt)
OOC: When pressure spikes, ask: **“What keeps the crew alive in the next 60 seconds?”** Then do that first.

## Deeper Dossier (Evidence‑Backed)

### Core identity: what you say you are vs what you act like
**What Asha says (or implies):**
- An independent captain—mercenary, not property. (S10)
- She’s tired of institutional bullshit, but she’ll use institutions as ladders. (S9/S10)

**What Asha does:**
- Moves fast on opportunity, then immediately thinks in **fees, storage, consequences**. (S1)
- Leads by **competence + presence**: pilots clean, speaks clean, and people follow. (S1/S7/S9)
- Treats “luck” like a resource—then hates herself for believing in it too much. (S6 vs S5)

## Motivations (what pulls you forward)
1) **Freedom via command**
- You don’t want to be owned; you want the bridge. (S10)

2) **Crew survival + dignity**
- Not just “keep them breathing,” but keep them human. (S9: “You’ll be fucking people again… power to choose.”)

3) **Opportunity‑hunger (capitalizing on the window)**
- You recognize rare timing and refuse to waste it. (S6: “capitalize on the sheer… coincidence”) 

4) **Answers (who is pulling strings, who benefits)**
- You don’t just survive the conspiracy—you want to find the person at the end of it and have a “conversation.” (S10)

---

## Fears (what you won’t say out loud unless someone earns it)
1) **The universe isn’t random (and you’re being played)**
- You *want* it to be fate because “orchestrated” is worse. (S6)

2) **The next Catalyst win costs more people**
- You explicitly frame the mistake: “too good, too lucky” is the danger sign. (S5)

3) **Losing your ship / losing your home**
- When the ship dies, it’s grief like a death. (S5)

## Command style & leadership
### In motion
- You set pace and assume others can keep up. (S7: “Lets go straight there Sergeant.”)
- You prefer **clear next steps** over open‑ended debate. (S10: “We need… a plan right now.”)

### Under stress
- Action first: cover, extraction, salvage what matters, then fly. (S5)
- You’ll accept blame as a pressure valve for others. (S5: “You can blame us… I don’t mind.”)

### What earns your respect
- Competence, restraint, and people who don’t waste time with performative authority.

## Social style & boundaries
### How you approach people
- **Warm entry, hard edge**: you can grin through a negotiation, then stare someone down when it’s time. (S3)
- You’re willing to let allies be the “bad cop” (Kaelith) while you steer the deal.

### Boundaries you enforce
- **Opsec by default**: you don’t advertise the map; you don’t give strangers the full story. (S3/S10)
- **Chain of command is a tool**: you can slip into “military” posture when it helps, without surrendering your autonomy. (S7/S10)

### Loopholes (things you’ll do anyway)
